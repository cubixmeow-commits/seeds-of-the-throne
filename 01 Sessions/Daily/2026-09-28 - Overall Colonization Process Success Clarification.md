# Overall Colonization Process Success Clarification

## Author correction

The **overall two-planet colonization process is successful**. Planet Two's criminal groups lose a real Great War, but their defeat is **not** a failure of the governing colonization process. Planet Three's more contained replay is part of a successful continuing process, not an emergency repair of an unsuccessful first planet.

An assistant interpretation on September 28 called Planet Two's war a “real failure of the looser process” and Planet Three “the response.” **Reject that interpretation.** “Less contained” compares criminal latitude on the two planets; it does not mean Planet Two's governance, settlement, AI management or purposes failed. The war's occurrence does not by itself prove that the governing leaders intended, required or engineered that war. The author has established process success, not the exact measures by which each planet's success is judged.

## Preserved story facts

- Planet Two has the real Great War, the real leaders' victorious empire and the criminals' defeat.
- Planet Three begins a distinct process that replays selected history through placed people and roles under tighter containment for roughly a century before Sylvan. Its events and people are real; the empire does not physically transfer.
- The criminal leaders' plan, Konrad's Daemon, George and Aiden's enhancement attempts, and Samuel's eventual campaign can fail, injure people or provoke consequences **inside a successful overall process**. Their failures do not define the governing process's outcome.
- The accepted participant commitment, separate exit rights, physical endgame placements, and terminal sequence remain intact.

## Working design implication, not additional canon

We should construct the process as a **deliberate multi-stage colonization program** whose Planet Two and Planet Three stages have different purposes and permissions. The causal reason for a replay, the AI's exact responsibilities, the safety thresholds, the definition of success, and the ethical evaluation of what happened are still open. Planet Three may refine containment and cultivate successor bonds because the program progresses, not because its first colony must be declared broken.
