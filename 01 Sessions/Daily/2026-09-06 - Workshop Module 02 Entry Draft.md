# Workshop Module 02 — Entry Draft

## Gate

What did each class of participant knowingly accept before entry, and what can they refuse afterward?

## Author answer — verbatim

> They accepted the opportunity to participate, can exit at any time, and can trade in their results for their inheritance, the older leaders give them a chance, to become self made, in place of inheritance

## Exact restatement — author-accepted

The legitimate heirs enter voluntarily. The older leaders offer them an opportunity to become self-made through the colonization process instead of beginning with their inheritance. They may exit at any time and return to the inheritance path, but doing so means trading away the results, placement, or independent standing they were developing through the process.

This voluntary exit rule applies to the heirs and other voluntary trainees covered by the author's answer. It does not automatically release convicted criminals from court-assigned containment. The entry and refusal rights of locally born descendants remain unresolved.

## Workshop interpretation

- **Selected direction:** a modified combination of informed voluntary entry and a continuing right to exit.
- **Core tradeoff:** inherited position remains available, but the process gives an heir the chance to build an independently earned future instead.
- **Meaning of exit:** leaving is allowed; the cost is giving up the developing process result rather than being physically trapped until completion.
- **Class boundary:** court-assigned containment and voluntary development require different entry and exit rules.

## Scene test — non-canon

After a serious setback, an heir asks to leave. An official confirms that departure is available and that the heir can return to the inheritance path. What cannot travel with the heir is the unfinished claim to a self-made placement produced by the process. A convicted participant then demands the same exit, forcing the institution to explain the difference between voluntary training and a containment sentence.

## Adversarial result

The ability to return to an inheritance does not make participation meaningless. It makes the process a voluntary alternative to inherited power. Its result matters because it can establish an independent position, history, competence record, and placement that inheritance alone cannot provide. The exact benefits and what must be surrendered on exit remain unresolved.

## Remaining uncertainty

- What exactly is traded away when an heir exits: accumulated placement, assets, credentials, relationships, or only an unfinished evaluation?
- Can an heir leave and later re-enter?
- How much scenario information is knowingly accepted before entry?
- What refusal rights exist after awakening but before a new developmental stage?
- Which appeal, review, or sentence-completion routes exist for convicted participants?
- What rights and obligations apply to descendants born inside the process?

## Compound Writing adaptation test

### Tracks pass

The author's answer contains natural conversational compression but no development-process residue. The restatement removes no substantive idea. Workshop analysis, class boundaries, and unresolved questions remain outside the proposed canon statement, where they belong.

### Pressure-turn placement

The most important choice in the answer is the exchange between inherited position and a self-made result. The original wording places that relationship at the end. The restatement moves it to the beginning, then explains the continuing right to exit and its cost.

The pass adds useful clarity because “can exit at any time” could otherwise sound consequence-free. The reordered version shows that exit remains free while the two paths still produce different futures.
