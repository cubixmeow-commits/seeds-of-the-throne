# 2026-11-19: Mental Illness and Villainy, the Refined Rule

Author decision (Noa, 2026-11-19), refining the project's earlier framing.

The earlier compiled line said mental illness is never the engine of villainy. That was too absolute, and the author has replaced it.

The refined rule: mental illness can be the engine of villainy, but not in an absolute way. Samuel Franklin has sexual perversion and obsession, a type of mental illness, and it is driving him to villainy. But not all people with sexual perversion and obsession are villains. That distinction is the important thing, and the work must keep it visible.

In practice:
- A specific person's condition can be his engine. Samuel's is.
- A condition never stands as a verdict on a category of people. The same condition in another person does not produce Samuel.
- Diagnosis is neither automatic guilt nor automatic excuse. Samuel's condition explains his drive; his verdict rests on his choices and conduct, which is what the containment record exists to establish.
