# RW-10 Permanent Empire Bait

## Question explored

Which unresolved human question sends the reader from Book One into the earlier history?

## Author decisions

**Established:** the primary group of real leaders deliberately baited Konrad's contained leadership with a genuine possibility. If they teamed up with Samuel and successfully took Sylvan under the governing rules, they would gain permanent control of the largest empire.

**Established:** the prize is limited to the largest empire. It does not include the colonization planet as a whole, the overall colonization process, the larger humanity, or authority to rewrite the process rules.

**Established:** when the attempt to take Sylvan fails, the permanent-imperial path closes. The group's only remaining constructive option is to stop the rampage and cooperate with Sylvan and the legitimate leaders in order to receive control of a city.

**Established:** the deadline for taking Sylvan is Sylvan's forty-second birthday. If the group has not completed the governing victory condition by that birthday, the permanent-largest-empire path fails and closes.

**Established:** this supersedes the earlier active description of the Sylvan victory condition as wholly false and the description of cooperation as the only path from the beginning. Samuel's unsupported fantasy of using Sylvan to unlock the planet and conquer the wider process remains false. Any promise that the failed imperial opportunity can still be recovered is also false.

**Established:** Samuel gets Konrad to remain in containment by lying about what Sylvan and his new-generation Luminai really are. He makes the genuine imperial bait appear achievable on terms Konrad's worldview and older Daemon can dominate. Konrad's confidence that his Daemon can contain Samuel and outperform the successor bond remains part of the choice, but Samuel's false account of Sylvan and the Luminai is what turns that confidence into continued participation.

The primary leaders make the bounded imperial prize real because a merely imaginary offer would prove less. Konrad, Samuel, George, and the older group leaders receive a consequential opportunity to reveal what they choose when domination appears genuinely obtainable. Their failure therefore cannot be dismissed as resistance to a reward that never existed.

## Reverse-series handoff

Book One resolves Samuel's present action, but exposes an earlier choice that later books must dramatize:

> Why did the primary leaders offer the contained group permanent control of the largest empire, and what did each leader choose when the bait became real?

Readers know the group ultimately fails. The unresolved drama is how the offer was constructed, why the real leaders considered it safe and necessary, what Samuel told Konrad about Sylvan and the Luminai, why Konrad accepted Samuel as the required partner and stayed in containment, which members participated or resisted, and which choices created the century-long path to Sylvan.

## Preserved boundaries

- Samuel never controls the overall process.
- The real leaders and embedded process rules remain above the bounded imperial contest.
- Samuel's living-god fantasy and proposed conquest of the planet and larger humanity have no sound mechanism.
- Sylvan's forty-second birthday is the fixed deadline. Failure by that point closes the imperial route; it cannot be restored through escalation, reinterpretation, or another bargain.
- Cooperation after failure offers a city, not the largest empire.
- The exact false description Samuel gives Konrad of Sylvan and the Luminai remains unresolved.
- The exact legal transfer condition, meaning of “take Sylvan,” deadline-time convention, city-placement terms, safeguards, and reason the leaders could safely make permanent imperial control available remain unresolved.
- The story grounds culpability in voluntary conduct, coercion, ideology, and responsibility rather than mental illness, identity, ancestry, or harmless interests.
