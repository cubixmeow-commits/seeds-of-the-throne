# Supremacist Godhood Applied to Synthetic Embodiment

## Author's thematic connection

The villains' older supremacist obsession with an allegedly superior inherited identity is redirected toward synthetic embodiment: Samuel and Konrad imagine that taking synthetic bodies and capabilities would make them enhanced human gods. This is the meeting point between their fascist ideology and the colony's real synthetic technology. It explains why the existence of powerful biological synthetics is so provocative to them and why they exploit children, lineage, and supposed enhancement as part of a single project.

## Causal and ethical boundary

Their claim of a superior lineage and their mixed-ancestry theory are **false**; neither ancestry nor the birth of a child supplies scientific evidence of human–synthetic compatibility. The story can show how an invented hierarchy motivates recruitment, exploitation, concealment, and the attempted seizure of synthetic bodies without adopting the hierarchy as fact. Synthetic people are not a superior caste, an upgrade prize, or vacant shells. Whether a biological brain could ever inhabit an engineered synthetic body remains an open technical and moral question. Refer to the historical analogy at the level of ideology and mechanism; do not map fictional factions onto real historical nations or identities.

## Reveal value

An early reading may frame the breeding program as a bizarre obsession with inheritance or a promise of superior heirs. Later evidence reframes its founders' real objective: not simply descendants at the top, but the founders themselves seeking permanent synthetic embodiment and absolute authority. That shift does not excuse the earlier abuses or make the experiments scientifically productive.
