# Two-Planet Colonization Architecture Assessment

## Question explored

How can the first and second colonization planets form one intelligible historical process for participants who cannot bypass assigned environments, while preserving different rights of exit, the real Planet Two Great War, Planet Three's replay and the existing Sylvan endgame?

This is a synthesis of existing vault decisions plus an **unaccepted working design**. It does not establish new author canon.

## Current author-established frame

- Planet One is the origin world; Planet Two is the first colonization planet; Planet Three is the second and Sylvan's world. See [[07 QA/Decisions]] and [[02 Story/Components/Bridge World and Luminai Initialization]].
- Planet Two contains the comparatively less contained criminal groups, a real Great War, their defeat and the real leaders' victorious empire. Planet Three begins a separate process that **replays history through placed people and roles** under tighter containment for roughly a century before Sylvan. The original empire and its sovereignty do not simply migrate. [[01 Sessions/Daily/2026-09-28 - Planet Three Replay Clarification]].
- Both environments are physical societies; their people, institutions, actions and consequences are real. Surface histories may be curated, but the governing and sealed records remain separate from a criminal's public interpretation. [[02 Story/Systems/Story Functionality and Narrative Containment]] and [[03 Context/WORLD]].
- While a participant continues in the process, they cannot skip, abandon or rewrite an assigned environment. A voluntary heir may instead leave the **whole** process and relinquish its unfinished result. Convicted criminals follow distinct adjudication and release rules; locally born descendants' rights remain unresolved. [[07 Coordination/Story Completion Workflow/Workshop/02 - Participant knowledge and entry]] and [[07 Coordination/Story Completion Workflow/Workshop/07 - Grid permissions and failure]].
- After defeat Konrad has an available lawful exit from his exceptional claim and **chooses** continued participation; that choice does not bind each follower automatically. [[07 Coordination/Story Completion Workflow/Workshop/09 - Great War defeat and refusal to exit]].
- Samuel and Konrad are two leaders of a shared criminal project and stage enmity; Samuel later plans to blame Konrad. This can span both worlds without making the Planet Two war fictitious. [[02 Story/Systems/Shared Colonization Process and Rotating Roles]].
- George and Aiden act physically on Planet Three during Sylvan's endgame. Older notes place Samuel and Konrad physically on Planet Two then. The exact travel, authority and cross-world command channels remain open. [[02 Story/Components/Book One Outcome Presentation and Reverse Discovery]].

## Working whole-process architecture

| Layer | Planet Two: first process | Planet Three: successor replay |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Civilizational purpose | Establish a living colony and observe the first bounded encounter among real settlers, synthetics, ambitious builders, heirs and dangerous factions. The exact initial mix is open. | Build another real society using lessons and selected source history while cultivating heirs, testing successor human–Luminai bonds and improving criminal containment. |
| Criminal opportunity | Comparatively loose conditions let several groups grow, recruit, coordinate and challenge other institutions. Their actual war ends in defeat. | Tighter permissions allow consequential local roles and renewed attempts, but preserve the governing process and independent records beyond any criminal's command. |
| What carries forward | A *record* of actual events, living people where individually authorized, technical lessons, unresolved harms and criminal beliefs. | Placed people, curated roles and historical correspondences can initially suggest continuity; later events are new acts by real participants. No original empire's sovereign title transfers by default. |
| Social reality | The victorious empire and defeated movement are actual Planet Two institutions; the war is not a simulation. | Dominant and defeated-side positions are newly situated Planet Three institutions/roles. The amount of reproduced history is still an author gate. |
| End condition | Military loss ends the less-contained criminal project as a viable claim of victory; Konrad's lawful exit and refusal are distinct from the war result. | Roughly a century of containment, rotating offices, separate altered realities and the Sylvan–Orzai endgame lead to defined presentations and terminal consequences. |

**Proposed governing distinction:** use a common outer colonization authority and sealed record across the two planets, but separate **assignment contracts, local institutions, permission budgets and outcome criteria**. The common authority is plausible from prior process rules; its legal charter, human composition, audit independence and cross-world enforcement are not yet established. This distinction keeps the two planets connected without treating Planet Three as a migrated state or a scripted reenactment.

## Working chronological spine, with no invented dates

1. **Origin and inducement:** a leading private-corporate colonization venture, synthetic breakthrough, false ascension movement and eventual law/containment response require an exact order. There may be constructive founders and culpable promoters in different categories.
2. **Planet Two initialization:** the first colony becomes a real society. The comparatively loose criminal permissions, entry terms and safeguards need definition. Genuine civilization-building cannot be reduced to a test population.
3. **Planet Two escalation:** multiple criminal groups and the Samuel–Konrad joint project become publicly legible as opposing camps. The relationship among real rivalry, staged rivalry, exile and sabotage remains open.
4. **Real Great War and defeat:** the criminals lose to the real leaders' empire. The war's costs and individuals' acts remain in the sealed history regardless of later stories.
5. **Postwar choice and allocation:** Konrad may accept a lawful end to his exceptional claim but chooses to remain. Each leader, follower, heir and locally born person needs a distinct continuation/release/placement rule. Samuel's earlier jurisdiction and Konrad's later reactivation need a dated planet and permission chain.
6. **Planet Three initialization:** real leaders open a separate, more contained process. It places George, Aiden, legitimate heirs and other participants into roles drawing on Planet Two history. Decide which archives, institutions, lineages and events are copied, adapted or merely alluded to.
7. **Overlapping century:** Planet Two can continue to hold older leaders while Planet Three's society develops. Cross-world influence, physical travel, communication, transfer of evidence, and the timing of George and Aiden's placements need specific rules. Samuel exploits the replay's apparent political contrast and the generation gap.
8. **Sylvan endgame:** Book One begins near the outcome; the Resistance and participant choices distinguish the actual Planet Two war, Planet Three placements, the shared criminal project, Samuel's later unilateral abuses and authentic successor bonds. Preserve the accepted terminal sequence without silently moving Samuel or Konrad.

## Candidate replay scopes — alternatives, not canon

| Model | What it reproduces | Dramatic advantage | Main risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Roles and institutional patterns** | Offices, empire identities and social pressures, with new decisions and divergent events. | Clear separation between real Planet Two war and Planet Three's own history. | Requires a credible account of why roles still resemble the original so closely. |
| **Curated historical starting state** | Selected archives, political settlements, biographies and organizations as the opening conditions. | Creates strong audience misdirection and evidence-based revelation. | False records must not erase consent, individual rights or the sealed truth. |
| **Broad civilizational reconstruction** | A longer technological and social arc modeled on the first world's history. | Makes the two planets a meaningful generational comparison. | A repeated Great War can accidentally become a second canonical war and make ordinary inhabitants seem scripted. |

**Working recommendation for assessment:** start with roles and institutional patterns, add only selected curated records whose custody and purpose the plot requires, and let Planet Three diverge through its residents' choices. This limits contradictory war chronology and protects the world's reality. The author has not selected a model.

## What “locked into” can mean without rewriting accepted rights

The accepted rule is **environment commitment during continuing participation**. It does not mean every person is imprisoned on both planets or must physically move to the next one. Voluntary heirs can leave the overall process at a cost to their unfinished result; convicted criminals have separate lawful review and release; Konrad's postwar continuation is a consequential choice; locally born people must not silently inherit a criminal sentence. A further author decision is needed if “locked into both planets” intends a stronger personal or interplanetary obligation than this.

## Proposed audience-reveal ladder

1. Planet Three's surface history presents George's dominant role and a defeated-side inheritance as if the public political division explains the whole conflict.
2. Independently authenticated Planet Two records establish where the actual war and victory occurred; Planet Three is a successor process with selected replay placements.
3. Placement authorizations and contradictory participant accounts identify the real differences between source events and Planet Three roles.
4. Shared Samuel–Konrad records, failed enhancement evidence and testimony of affected people expose the joint project and Samuel's later framing campaign.
5. Formal endgame evidence distinguishes each person's conduct, rather than treating a replayed office, family identity or public legend as proof of guilt.

The series may **present** these layers out of chronological order. Book One's outcome frame and later reverse discovery remain intact.

## Highest-impact open questions

1. What is the entry/exit/transfer status of **each class**: convicted founders, voluntary heirs, followers, synthetics, locally born residents and children of contained leaders?
2. Which Planet Two people actually cross to Planet Three, on whose authority and at what time? Which older leaders stay physically on Planet Two while acting through channels or proxies?
3. Does Planet Three replay only roles and institutional pressures, or also public archives and events? What can the residents discover about the source history?
4. What was Planet Two's prewar containment boundary, and what measurable rule becomes stricter on Planet Three?
5. Where and when does Konrad reactivate his Daemon and group inside Samuel's priority sphere: Planet Two, Planet Three, or a cross-world arrangement? The existing causal result is accepted; its new geography is not.
6. How can the same governing process protect independent people on both worlds while allowing real local power, wrongdoing and later review?

## Continuity findings

- **Blocking for a detailed historical outline:** Older dated timeline sections still mix the construction and integration of Planet Three with the pre-Planet Two Great War sequence. Treat those sections as historical working reconstructions, not dated fact.
- **Significant:** [[02 Story/Systems/The Planet]] says numerous earlier successful colonies despite the later explicit three-world map; later author decision controls.
- **Significant:** Earlier material says the planet reconstructs the parent civilization's distant past, while the September 28 correction says Planet Three replays Planet Two. These can be combined as layers only after deciding what each source contributes.
- **Significant:** Literal cross-world transfer of the victorious empire and defeated groups was an earlier assistant inference, explicitly corrected. Physical movements of individual people remain possible but unassigned.
- **Significant:** “Locked in” must be scoped to assignment, voluntary departure, convicted status and children's independent rights. A blanket no-exit rule conflicts with accepted modules 02, 07 and 09.
