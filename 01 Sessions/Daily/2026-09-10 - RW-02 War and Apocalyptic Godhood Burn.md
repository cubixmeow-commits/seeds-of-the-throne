# RW-02 War and Apocalyptic Godhood Burn

## Question explored

What categories of action let Samuel credibly consume $15 million within days while trying to control the colonization process?

## Existing constraints

- Samuel receives the allocation only after formal separation.
- He has lost the process-provided systems previously used to attack Sylvan.
- The allocation is genuinely available for an independent attempt rather than secretly rigged against him.
- Samuel is highly intelligent and understands individual costs; his obsession corrupts the objective rather than making him suddenly incompetent.
- The entire allocation is exhausted within days.

## Possibilities considered

The workshop initially considered purchased access, competing operations, escalating prices, and a self-financed demonstration of control. The author's answer gave these mechanisms a stronger dramatic purpose: Samuel is trying to sustain a war, present himself as a living god, and force an apocalyptic culmination.

The author's reference to something similar to the Book of Revelation is a temporary real-world development analogy. Compiled story notes reduce it to an invented apocalyptic mechanism and do not import a real religious prophecy into canon.

## Author decisions

**Established:** Samuel's primary spending engine is his attempt to keep the terminal war alive after the process has already reached conclusion. He spends his finite independent resources sustaining operations that no longer have process-provided support.

**Established:** the secondary accelerant is his attempt to manufacture an apocalyptic culmination in which escalating conflict, apparent signs, catastrophe, and his promised intervention force the civilization to recognize him as a living god. He is not merely trying to win a remaining battle. He is trying to make the ending of the war validate his divinity and place the colonization process beneath him.

Because the manufactured culmination requires simultaneous escalation, continued conflict, visible proof, and a promised act of deliverance or transformation, Samuel cannot conserve resources without undermining his own claim. Failed signs or interventions do not cause him to abandon the theory. He interprets them as incomplete execution and commits more of the remaining allocation.

## Current working direction

The resource collapse is the material form of Samuel's godhood obsession:

`keep the war alive -> manufacture apocalyptic signs and crisis -> promise divine culmination -> failed validation -> larger escalation -> rapid exhaustion`

He can accurately understand what each operation costs and still be unable to stop because standing down would concede that the war is over, the prophecy is false, and he is an ordinary contained criminal being measured without borrowed power.

Exact weapons, personnel, infrastructure, propaganda channels, staged signs, daily totals, and physical scale remain scene-level work. They must fit the available independent economy and cannot restore the systems revoked at separation.

## Unresolved questions

- What visible event does Samuel promise will complete his apocalyptic claim?
- How much of the terminal war is physical conflict, information conflict, institutional coercion, or several bounded fronts?
- Who still participates voluntarily, who is coerced, and what protections prevent Samuel's final expenditure from becoming unlimited harm?
- Which remaining observers initially believe the promised culmination?
- How are the expenditures authenticated for RW-03 and RW-06?
