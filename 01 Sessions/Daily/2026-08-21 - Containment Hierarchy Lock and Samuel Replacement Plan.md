# Containment Hierarchy Lock and Samuel Replacement Plan

## Accepted structural rule

The containment hierarchy **locks when the first primary contained criminal is integrated into it**.

This means the identity and position of the first primary criminal placed into the hierarchy has long-term structural consequences. Once the hierarchy locks, later primary contained criminals enter relative to that already-established structure rather than freely redefining it.

The exact technical and institutional implementation remains unresolved, but the narrative rule is now accepted.

## Samuel's deception

Samuel knows or discovers that this hierarchy-lock rule matters before Konrad is fully integrated into containment.

Because of his exile, humiliation, and growing desire to replace Konrad rather than return beneath him, Samuel lies about how the hierarchy works.

He allows or causes Konrad to believe that Samuel is merely restoring access, rebuilding Konrad's organization, or helping Konrad reestablish control inside containment.

In reality, Samuel is trying to ensure that **Samuel himself becomes the first primary contained criminal integrated into the relevant hierarchy**, causing the structure to lock around Samuel's position rather than Konrad's.

Konrad thinks Samuel is giving him back his empire.

In reality, Konrad has migrated the machinery of his organization into Samuel's jurisdiction **after the hierarchy has already been structured around Samuel**.

## Why this matters to Samuel

The deception is not merely opportunistic.

It grows directly from exile.

Konrad's movement cast Samuel out of a hierarchy Samuel believed should have belonged to him by inheritance and proximity. Samuel's later plan is therefore not simply to survive containment or regain status.

He wants to reverse the original exclusion by making the new contained hierarchy recognize **him** as structurally primary.

This is the beginning of Samuel's replacement project:

`excluded from Konrad's hierarchy -> enters containment first -> lies about hierarchy-lock mechanics -> secures primary position -> convinces Konrad to reactivate/migrate old programs into Samuel's domain -> uses Konrad's own organizational machinery from inside Samuel's jurisdiction -> gradually replaces Konrad functionally and symbolically`

## Konrad's misunderstanding

Konrad does not believe Samuel has become his legitimate superior.

Konrad may still regard Samuel as a pathetic outsider, failed son, vulgar subordinate, or technically useful loser.

What Konrad misunderstands is the **containment architecture**.

He believes Samuel's local control is merely infrastructure that can be used to restore Konrad's old programs and authority.

He does not realize that Samuel has already used the lock condition to establish the hierarchy in a way that makes those restored programs operate under Samuel's structurally prior containment position.

Konrad therefore participates in his own displacement.

He authorizes or reactivates the machinery of his former empire while believing he is reclaiming it.

Instead, he is rebuilding it inside a hierarchy that has already been locked around Samuel.

## Strong thematic symmetry

The Fitzgerald order originally controls Samuel through a flattering lie about status:

**You are not being exiled; you are being entrusted with an important mission.**

Samuel later controls Konrad through another flattering lie about status:

**You are not rebuilding beneath me; I am merely helping you restore what was always yours.**

In both cases, the victim cooperates because the false explanation preserves his sense of importance.

Samuel uses against Konrad the same psychological mechanism once used against Samuel.

## Story consequences

- Samuel does not need broad planetary authority to begin replacing Konrad.
- The key advantage is **sequence and lock order**, not omnipotence.
- Samuel's earlier presence in containment becomes causally essential rather than merely convenient.
- Konrad's decision to reactivate his programs becomes much more consequential because the hierarchy has already locked.
- Samuel's later control of genealogies, breeding systems, certification, internal communications, and organizational dependencies can grow from the fact that those systems were reactivated under Samuel's jurisdiction.
- Konrad can continue believing Samuel is socially beneath him even while Samuel has become structurally primary inside containment.
- Samuel's eventual Throne becomes the mature form of a replacement process that began with the first hierarchy lock.

## Unresolved questions

1. What exactly qualifies someone as the "first primary contained criminal" for hierarchy-lock purposes?
2. Does Samuel knowingly engineer his own integration first, or does he realize the opportunity after being integrated?
3. Who normally explains the lock rule to contained participants, and how does Samuel prevent Konrad from learning the truth?
4. What parts of the hierarchy become irreversible after the lock: authority order, program ownership, dependency routing, identity namespace, command priority, inheritance, or several of these?
5. Can the hierarchy ever be unlocked, or only superseded by an external legitimate authority?
6. Why do the containment designers use a first-primary lock at all? What legitimate safety, accountability, or containment function does it serve?
7. Which of Konrad's programs must be explicitly reauthorized by Konrad before Samuel can host them?
8. At what point does Konrad realize the hierarchy was locked around Samuel rather than himself?
9. Does Samuel understand from the beginning that he intends to replace Konrad, or does revenge evolve into replacement after he sees what the lock gives him?
10. How does the hierarchy-lock mechanism connect to Samuel's later claim to kingship and the Throne?
