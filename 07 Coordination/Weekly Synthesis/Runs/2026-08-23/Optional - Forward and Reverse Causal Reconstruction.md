# Optional Forward and Reverse Causal Reconstruction

## Forward chain

`beginning state -> choices -> events -> consequences -> ending state`

`Konrad builds hierarchical order -> Samuel receives privilege and commits misconduct -> Konrad disguises disposal as impossible mission -> Samuel and George choose wrongdoing and fail -> George enters containment and Samuel gains asymmetrical familiarity -> Konrad chooses conquest and rejects correction -> faction loses -> Konrad chooses Samuel's restoration interpretation -> machinery enters Samuel's jurisdiction -> Samuel chooses expanding revenge and replacement -> False Victory and genealogy system require constant maintenance -> Samuel targets Sylvan's development -> Sylvan chooses reality-testing through successive environments -> bond completes under pressure -> Samuel chooses continued intervention after restraint is safer -> interventions prove command -> Sylvan refuses to destroy George -> George faces first unmanufactured choice -> external domination ends and bounded stewardship becomes possible.`

## Reverse chain

Starting from the current final state, ask what must already be true, what evidence must exist, who must have acted, and what earlier setup must support each condition.

For Samuel to be publicly exposed as the active commander, observers need an authenticated record of interventions only the maintainer could initiate. That record requires a bounded terminal environment with pre-existing audit authority. Sylvan must reach it through earned survival and a capability demonstrated earlier. Samuel must believe one more intervention can still preserve the outcome. George must remain in a reality that needs servicing, and Samuel must benefit if George is destroyed.

For that maintenance trap to matter, the False Victory, Konrad's isolation, genealogy fraud, proxies, and George's Daemon must form an interconnected system whose layers can cascade. That system requires long access created after defeat. The access requires Konrad's authorization or migration into Samuel's earlier jurisdiction. The migration requires a hierarchy in which Samuel's primary status has real operational meaning and a defeated Konrad who needs restoration to be true.

For Konrad to accept restoration, he must have genuinely lost, lack a fallback, still despise Samuel, and receive a technically plausible offer that survives an attempted verification. His defeat must grow primarily from his own authoritarian choices, while Samuel's sabotage remains a material betrayal rather than the sole cause.

For Sylvan's refusal to distinguish him morally from Samuel, Sylvan must earlier learn that manipulated premises do not erase responsibility but do change the ethical response. Orzai, the Witness archive, the Luminai, and George's remaining agency can each contribute evidence or pressure, but none may decide for him.
