## Central author gate

> What may Samuel still choose, and which attempted consequence is physically or institutionally impossible?

Ask this question alone during actual use. Do not ask every follow-up at once.

## Possibilities and tradeoffs

### 1. Bounded communications and authority claims

**PROPOSED:** Samuel can bargain, lie and issue logged requests while harmful execution is blocked. Strong method exposure; enough human response must remain unscripted.

### 2. Protected proxy interactions

**PROPOSED:** Consenting protected intermediaries can accept or refuse limited approaches. Rich social adaptation; consent must be revocable and protection cannot rely on deception alone.

### 3. Controlled resource competition

**PROPOSED:** Samuel makes real allocation choices over strictly bounded resources. Shows priorities under pressure; cannot manufacture deprivation for uninformed people.

### 4. Successive intervention tests

**PROPOSED:** Sylvan interrupts each harmful escalation while leaving a safe next choice. Demonstrates adaptive containment; clear rules prevent it feeling like a rigged humiliation exercise.

## Targeted follow-up questions

- What new uncertainty does each year resolve?

## Scene test

**Non-canon situation:** Samuel’s next request would expose a person outside the agreed boundary; Sylvan interrupts it immediately, sacrificing a fuller pattern demonstration.

Record what each person wants, can know, can refuse, and can actually change. Name the observable difference between the selected alternatives.

## Adversarial questions

- What prevented this same safeguard from protecting earlier generations? Distinguish earlier failures, new capabilities and newly acquired authority.
- Which established constraint would this answer break, and what evidence would reveal that break before the payoff?
