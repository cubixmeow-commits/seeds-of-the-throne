# RW-10: Reverse-series handoff

## Purpose

Let Book One end completely while making the Great War, false victory, and generational capture dramatically necessary rather than supplementary explanation.

## Established

Book One begins near the chronological end. Samuel is processed and exposed. Sylvan and Orzai accept placement. Later books return to earlier events in the order that produces the strongest drama.

## Four possibilities

1. **The first voluntary concession:** who knowingly gave Samuel the access he later weaponized?
2. **The preserved warning:** who recognized the pattern early and failed, refused, or saved evidence?
3. **The cost of betrayal:** what did Samuel sacrifice when his plan was still strategic rather than compulsive?
4. **The captive's choice:** why did Konrad remain when he could have left after defeat?

## Central author gate

Which unresolved human question sends the reader from Book One into the earlier history?

Choose a question whose outcome remains emotionally uncertain even though readers know Samuel eventually loses.

## Scene test

The final pages close Samuel's present action, show Sylvan and Orzai beginning their placement, and reveal one earlier choice whose full meaning Book One cannot contain.

## Adversarial test

Do not withhold a fact Book One needs for its own resolution. The later book should deepen responsibility and contingency, not retroactively invalidate the ending.

## Accepted result — 2026-09-11

The reverse-series handoff is the primary leaders' genuine imperial bait. They made permanent control of the largest empire available if Konrad's group teamed with Samuel and successfully took Sylvan under the process rules. The prize never included the planet, the overall colonization process, or larger humanity.

The deadline is Sylvan's forty-second birthday. If the group has not successfully taken him under the governing rules by that birthday, the imperial path closes permanently. The group's only remaining constructive option is to stop the rampage and cooperate with Sylvan and the legitimate leaders for control of a city. This supersedes the earlier treatment of the entire Sylvan victory condition as a lie and the treatment of cooperation as the only available path from the beginning. Samuel's wider claim that Sylvan could unlock the planet and support his expansion into living godhood remains false, as does any later claim that the lost imperial opportunity can be restored.

Samuel gets Konrad to remain in containment by lying about what Sylvan and the new Luminai really are. He makes the real imperial bait appear achievable through the kind of domination Konrad and his experienced Daemon believe they understand. Konrad's Daemon wager therefore remains part of his motive, while Samuel's false account of the successor pair supplies the decisive reason to stay and attempt the bait.

The final human question is:

> Why did the primary leaders offer the contained group permanent control of the largest empire, and what did each leader choose when the bait became real?

The earlier books can answer how the offer worked, why the real leaders made it, what Samuel falsely told Konrad about Sylvan and the Luminai, why Konrad accepted Samuel as his partner and remained, who resisted or participated, and how those choices created the long path to Sylvan's forty-second birthday. Exact lie content, imperial transfer mechanics, deadline-time convention, city terms, and safeguards remain open.
