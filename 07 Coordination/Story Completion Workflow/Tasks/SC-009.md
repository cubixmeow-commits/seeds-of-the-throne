# SC-009 — Define a Hard Limit and Cost

## Objective

Define what the Sylvan–Luminai bond can never do and what meaningful cost, risk, requirement, or constraint accompanies use of its deeper human-mediated capabilities, so the bond remains powerful without replacing Sylvan's human choices.

## Accepted SC-009 macro findings

### Question 1 — accepted

The primary hard limit is **process-enforced human decision authority**.

Even after the Sylvan–Luminai pair becomes highly developed, the Luminai is subject to strict limits on what it may initiate, decide, or execute without a conscious decision from Sylvan.

The bond is designed to amplify human agency rather than replace it. At Macro Shape, the older leaders' intended architecture should be treated like a real safety-critical control system for extremely advanced AI:

- observation is distinct from recommendation;
- recommendation is distinct from action;
- reversible local action is distinct from irreversible or human-affecting action;
- increasingly consequential actions require increasingly explicit human authorization;
- authority transfers and important interventions should be auditable;
- autonomous behavior may exist only inside tightly bounded categories, especially protective or immediately reversible functions;
- responsibility for consequential choices remains with the human partner.

The exact permission ladder should later be designed systematically as though the older civilization had deliberately engineered a mature human–AI governance model, rather than invented ad hoc for individual scenes.

### Question 2 — accepted

Sylvan carries two linked burdens.

First, **final responsibility remains human**. The Luminai may perceive more, model faster, warn, recommend, and perform bounded lower-level processing, but consequential decisions still have to resolve through Sylvan's own conscious authority.

Second, the Luminai's internal activity is not psychologically invisible to him. Bounded sub-decisions and ongoing processing can occur below Sylvan's conscious awareness, but they are routed through the human side of the bond deeply enough that their affective consequences surface through his subconscious and into conscious experience.

Sylvan can therefore **feel the interactivity of decisions he did not consciously formulate**. Emotional pressure, instinctive reactions, unease, urgency, attraction, aversion, confidence, or other affective residues can rise into consciousness even when he cannot explicitly inspect every intermediate Luminai decision that produced them.

This does not give the Luminai covert authority over consequential choices. The process still reserves those decisions to Sylvan. Instead, it makes the partnership experientially real: Sylvan bears both the responsibility for final decisions and the emotional/instinctive load of the Luminai's ongoing bounded cognition.

## Macro result

The developed bond is powerful but structurally non-sovereign. The Luminai may observe, model, advise, communicate, and perform bounded lower-level actions, including some processing below Sylvan's conscious awareness, but process rules reserve consequential action to Sylvan's conscious decision authority. The human partner remains accountable and indispensable.

The cost is not merely administrative confirmation. Sylvan experiences the affective residue of the Luminai's subconscious-level processing, while still having to make and own the final consequential decisions himself.
