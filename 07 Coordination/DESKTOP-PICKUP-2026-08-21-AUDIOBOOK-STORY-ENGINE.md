## Author intent

The author wants to **integrate heavily the development principles that make the Expeditionary Force series read/listen so well**, especially for audiobook use.

This does **not** mean imitating Craig Alanson's prose voice, phrasing, recurring jokes, or distinctive style.

The target is the underlying storytelling engineering:

- immediate concrete problems;
- simple scene objectives inside a complex world;
- problem-solving as propulsion;
- discovery before explanation;
- exposition through action, disagreement, testing, and consequence;
- strong conversational character dynamics;
- competence as entertainment;
- `problem -> attempt -> result -> consequence -> revised problem` loops;
- successful workarounds that create larger problems;
- natural listener orientation;
- chapter endings built around changed state;
- personality and humor under pressure;
- recurring mechanisms with meaningful variation rather than repetitive formulas.

## Core principle

**Complex world, simple scene.**

Seeds can remain structurally deep and highly interconnected while each current scene gives the listener a clear immediate problem to hold onto.

## Structural integration requested for desktop session

Do not treat this only as a prose/audiobook polish layer.

Integrate the Problem-Solving Story Engine into the full development stack:

### Story Exploration Lab

Add evaluation for:

- Immediate Objective Clarity
- Problem-Solving Potential
- Escalation Potential
- Audiobook Legibility
- Solution-to-Consequence Payoff

Generated ideas should be preferred when they produce active scenes, decisions, attempts, failures, discoveries, and consequences rather than only clever lore.

### Story Gauntlet

Add a problem-solving/listener-clarity critic lens asking:

1. What does the viewpoint character want immediately?
2. What prevents it?
3. What do they currently believe?
4. What do they try?
5. What evidence changes their model?
6. What succeeds?
7. What fails?
8. What new problem emerges?
9. Can the audience follow the current sequence without understanding the entire background system?
10. Is exposition attached to choice, test, conflict, or consequence?

### Scene development

Before prose, identify:

`immediate problem -> current model -> constraint -> attempt -> result -> model update -> state change -> long-range setup/payoff`

This is a development aid, not a rigid formula.

### Prose / audiobook pass

Add a separate listening-first critic after prose generation:

- Could a listener follow while driving or walking?
- Is location clear?
- Is the immediate objective clear?
- Are speakers distinguishable?
- Does orientation get restored naturally when needed?
- Is technical information attached to action/conflict?
- Are new terms introduced at manageable density?
- Are important causal turns understandable on first hearing?
- Does the scene end in a changed state?

## Sylvan application

Strong recurring reader-facing engine:

`observe anomaly -> preserve evidence -> build model -> test -> get something wrong -> revise -> discover capability -> antagonist interferes -> old model fails -> identify deeper rule -> carry improved state forward`

This complements the established recursive Luminai reliability loop rather than replacing it.

The Luminai loop explains **how Sylvan thinks reliably**.

The Problem-Solving Story Engine explains **how that thinking becomes entertaining scenes**.

## Great War application

Potential institutional-scale engine:

`Konrad encounters obstacle -> hierarchy produces solution -> apparent success -> hidden institutional cost -> Samuel/enemy exploits weakness -> leadership misreads result -> next decision compounds problem`

This can turn the Great War from historical summary into a chain of clear scene-level problems while preserving the larger systemic collapse.

Samuel remains an accelerant, not the sole cause of Fitzgerald defeat.

## Samuel / Konrad containment application

The new engine fits the established containment foundation especially well.

Immediate Konrad problem after defeat:

**The machinery that made his authority real is inactive or inaccessible. He wants it restored.**

Samuel offers a workable path.

The restoration should appear successful enough that Konrad restores more.

Hidden causal structure:

`loss of genuine city-based control -> need to restore programs -> Samuel supplies path -> reactivation inside largest-empire containment environment -> hierarchy already locked around Samuel -> restored systems operate inside Samuel's jurisdiction -> dependency and surveillance consequences -> permanent Fitzgerald destruction`

Critical accepted hinge:

**Konrad thinks Samuel is giving him back his empire. In reality, Konrad has just migrated the machinery of his organization into Samuel's jurisdiction.**

Exact hierarchy mechanics remain for the dedicated piece-by-piece brainstorm. Do not invent them during integration.

## Development philosophy

The desired combined effect is:

**clear and propulsive on the surface, deep and interconnected underneath.**

A listener should be able to follow the current human problem easily while later discovering that seemingly small solutions, permissions, compromises, and workarounds were participating in much larger causal structures.
