# Story Sites Visual Direction Assessment

## Author decision — September 8

The author selected **Hidden Planetary Infrastructure** as the primary structural direction. The implementation will borrow the fractured-record and recovered-evidence language from the darker archive concept and preserve the balanced concept's editorial legibility. See [[01 Sessions/Daily/2026-09-08 - Hidden Planetary Infrastructure Website Direction]], [[07 Coordination/Website Redesign/2026-09-08 - Hidden Planetary Infrastructure Asset Manifest]], and [[07 Coordination/2026-09-08 - Cursor Hidden Planetary Infrastructure Website Redesign]].

This selection establishes website direction only. It does not establish the literal architecture or mechanisms depicted in generated concepts.

## Assessment

The site should feel like a recovered history being tested against a still-living containment system. It should not look like a generic dark software dashboard, generic fantasy promotion, or a decorative archive skin.

That direction follows the story's actual engine:

- an ordinary, historically convincing surface civilization exists above hidden advanced infrastructure;
- the civilization and the lives within it are real, not a fake simulation or disposable stage set;
- public history conflicts with sealed records and observable consequences;
- patterns may be meaningful, planted, inherited, misread, or exploited, so provenance and rival explanations matter;
- Samuel and Konrad represent different systems for using people as material;
- Sylvan's defining alternative is contact with reality, correction, consent, distributed authority, and restraint;
- Book One begins near the chronological end and exposes how the apparent history was constructed;
- the narrative mode is archive thriller / dark historical reconstruction: what people thought happened, what happened, and the detail that changes the meaning.

The central design question is therefore not “How do we make the site more cinematic?” It is: **How can the visitor feel the tension between an authoritative public record and the hidden evidence that reinterprets it, while still understanding the literal premise immediately?**

## The two sites need different jobs

| Surface | Visitor promise | Character | Primary action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Story atlas | Understand and enter *Seeds of the Throne* | Editorial, cinematic, human, mysterious | Follow the story, world, people, and concealed history |
| Project Explorer | See how a large story becomes author-approved finished work | Precise, inspectable, task-oriented | Follow conversation → evidence → decision → scene → prose |

They should share a visual world but not the same page grammar. The story atlas is the recovered history. Project Explorer is the instrument used to inspect and build it.

## First-screen requirement

A new visitor should be able to answer within roughly ten seconds:

1. What is this?
2. What is the human conflict?
3. Why does the hidden system matter?
4. Where should I go next?

The first screen should contain:

- the title;
- a plain two- or three-sentence explanation of the colonization planet, the human–Luminai relationship, and the training/containment purpose;
- one consequential story turn, not a substitute slogan;
- a strong story image or evidence composition;
- two clear actions: enter the story and see how it is being built.

Avoid a huge title surrounded by empty space. Explanation and the next action should be visible without scrolling on a normal laptop and remain coherent on a 320px screen.

## Visual thesis: historical surface, engineered understructure

### Surface layer

Use institutional portraiture, lived-in architecture, maps, correspondence, official records, ceremonial objects, family and organizational marks, and historically plausible materials. These elements should feel used by real people, not assembled as fantasy decoration.

### Hidden-system layer

Use precise grids, alignment marks, provenance trails, permission/status labels, orbital or moon-scale arcs, bounded fields, and quiet cyan/teal illumination. The system should feel ancient in duration and exact in operation, not like a contemporary spaceship HUD.

### Coercive intrusion

Use crimson sparingly for captured pathways, falsified authority, coercion, and contradiction. It should interrupt the established order rather than bathe every surface in red.

### Responsible alternative

Use clarity, warm light, ivory, and controlled cyan for Sylvan and constructive Luminai work. Avoid a simplistic hero-blue/glowing-orb language. The positive system should communicate legibility, correction, and shared agency.

## Recommended visual grammar

- **Serif:** story turns, names, recovered-document headings, and moments of historical weight.
- **Sans serif:** literal explanation, navigation, annotations, and accessible reading.
- **Monospace:** provenance, dates, status, references, permissions, and machine-visible traces.
- **Images:** cinematic evidence objects and human moments, not full-bleed wallpaper behind every section.
- **Layout:** editorial sequences with controlled asymmetry; fewer, stronger panels rather than a card grid for every concept.
- **Motion:** restrained layer reveals, evidence alignment, and before/after interpretation. Avoid constant pulsing, glow, scanning lines, or ornamental HUD motion.
- **Texture:** physical records meeting exact system geometry. Keep texture subordinate to legibility.

Useful recurring motifs include marginal annotations, redaction/layer reveals, record discrepancies, lineage and influence lines, containment boundaries, archival stamps used with restraint, and orbital arcs that imply hidden planetary scale.

## Homepage narrative architecture

After the literal hero, guide the visitor through a five-part editorial sequence:

1. **A constructed world that became real** — an ordinary civilization with genuine history and agency.
2. **The hidden relationship** — humans discover and develop alongside Luminai rather than merely operating a tool.
3. **The contained leaders** — the system develops responsible leadership and contains people who instrumentalize others.
4. **The false public history** — Konrad's counterfeit victory, Samuel's quieter capture, and records that do not agree.
5. **The final exposure** — private doubt becomes common knowledge and the apparent endgame is reinterpreted.

This is a visitor path, not permission to publish unresolved plot detail. Use established public material and keep protected outcomes abstract where required.

## Page-specific direction

### Story

Present the series as reverse discovery: begin near the apparent end, expose a contradiction, then reveal why earlier history must be revisited. A compact sequence diagram or paired chronology/reading-order device would communicate this better than a set of equal cards.

### World

Use a layered cross-section: ordinary public civilization above construction history, environmental intelligence, and moon-scale command infrastructure. Keep surface institutions visually substantial so the design never implies that ordinary life is fake.

### Luminai

Center relationship, judgment, mutual correction, and different generations of integration. Do not default to robot portraits, glowing brains, or generic AI circuitry. The visual question is how two forms of mind learn to act without erasing either one's agency.

### Characters

Move beyond a uniform card gallery. Use portraits and artifacts within a relationship map organized around competing uses of power: domination, private compromise, evidence and patience, and distributed authority. Preserve locked character identities, especially Konrad's approved late-containment appearance.

### Conspiracy

Make this the clearest expression of public record versus sealed evidence. Show claims, sources, discrepancies, rival explanations, and consequences. Never present a pattern as proof merely because it repeats.

### Timeline

Show two related axes: chronological history and the order in which the reader discovers it. This directly expresses the Book One architecture and later backward movement without requiring a lore dump.

### Development pages

Ideas, Progress, Workshop, Research, Visual Archive, and Project Explorer should be clearly grouped as development, not mixed into the story journey as if they were chapters. Research needs an obvious route from the primary interface.

## Project Explorer direction

Project Explorer should feel like a working instrument from the same conceptual world: denser, calmer, and more exact than the story atlas. Its core demonstration should be a visible production chain:

`conversation → organized evidence → missing question → author decision → propagated updates → scene plan → prose and revision`

Its main views should each have a distinct job:

| View | Job |
|---|---|
| Overview | Explain the system and show the current production chain |
| Story | Present the organized story model and source-backed understanding |
| Decisions | Show open gates, accepted decisions, propagation, and consequences |
| Workshop | Ask one high-leverage question at a time and preserve author control |
| Progress | Show movement toward finished work, not repository activity alone |
| Files | Provide secondary source inspection without dominating the experience |

On mobile, prioritize the current task, decision, and progress. Move repository browsing behind a deliberate drawer or browse mode. The filesystem is supporting evidence, not the product's opening argument.

## Navigation model

Use two understandable groups in desktop and mobile navigation:

- **Story:** Story, World, Luminai, Characters, Conspiracy, Timeline.
- **Development:** Ideas, Progress, Workshop, Research, Visual Archive, Project Explorer / How It’s Built.

Every destination needs a truthful current state. The mobile hamburger should preserve the grouping rather than present one undifferentiated list.

## What to avoid

- generic black SaaS dashboard styling;
- generic fantasy parchment and ornamental medievalism;
- giant headings and empty cinematic space in place of explanation;
- a card grid as the default structure for every page;
- decorative red/cyan noise, constant glow, or fake system telemetry;
- a lore dump before the literal premise;
- treating technical source plumbing as the public story;
- visual language suggesting the world or its inhabitants are fake;
- turning unresolved questions, candidate images, or research into canon;
- another whole-site implementation before the author chooses a visual direction.

## Design acceptance tests

- A first-time visitor can explain the premise, conflict, and next action after the first screen.
- The story site feels like recovered history under live scrutiny, not project management software.
- Project Explorer demonstrates an authoring process, not merely a filesystem.
- Surface life feels historically real while the hidden system feels precise and consequential.
- Samuel, Konrad, Sylvan, Orzai, and the Luminai remain visually and ethically distinct.
- Mobile layouts are deliberately composed, not desktop sections stacked in source order.
- Plain language remains the foundation; atmosphere never replaces explanation.
- Locked visual identities and all source-authority boundaries remain intact.

## Recommended redesign process

1. Repair the current navigation and mobile defects using [[07 Coordination/2026-09-08 - Cursor Project Explorer Navigation Repair]].
2. Review this narrative and information-architecture assessment with the author.
3. Create two or three genuinely different visual-direction boards or static homepage concepts. Do not write the full site yet.
4. Select one direction and produce mobile/desktop wireframes for the homepage, one story page, and Project Explorer Overview.
5. Define the chosen tokens, typography, image rules, motifs, navigation, and responsive behavior.
6. Implement those representative surfaces and review them in-browser at all required widths.
7. Only after approval, roll the system across the remaining pages.

The strongest initial concept to test is **Recovered History / Living System**: a historically grounded editorial surface interrupted by precise evidence and containment layers. Alternative concepts should change composition and emphasis, not abandon the story's literal premise.
