# Obsession, Expectation, and Desperation Reversal

Samuel and George survive their earlier fall into containment by learning how to manipulate the other contained criminals through three vulnerabilities: obsession, expectation, and desperation.

They tell people what those people most need to believe. They anticipate the outcome the group is already hoping for, then use fear of losing that outcome to keep the group compliant. Over time, Samuel and George come to treat this as proof that they understand everyone else better than everyone else understands them.

Their final downfall should mirror that method.

Sylvan does not defeat them with an unrelated trick. He recognizes that Samuel and George are vulnerable to the same mechanism they used against others. Their obsession is to trap Sylvan, strip away his control, humiliate him, and prove they can dominate him. Their expectations tell them what that victory should look like. Their desperation makes them willing to ignore danger once the opportunity appears real.

Sylvan gives them a believable path toward that expected victory. They enter willingly because they think they are operating Sylvan's containment. Their participation exposes their methods, collaborators, betrayals, hidden infrastructure, and prior crimes.

The symmetry is the point:

- They built influence by understanding what desperate people will believe.
- They assumed they were exempt from the same vulnerability.
- Sylvan understands what they are obsessed with, what they expect to happen, and how desperate they have become to finally defeat him.
- The same psychological mechanism that once helped them control other contained criminals becomes the mechanism of their own containment and exposure.

This should function as a major endgame principle rather than a detached twist. Their downfall emerges directly from the method they used to survive and dominate earlier containment.
