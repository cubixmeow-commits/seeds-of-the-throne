# Journal Evidence and Forensics Integration

## Evidence contract

A journal is not automatically truthful or authentic. For any evidentiary use, record:

- claimant and claimed author;
- object and entry ID;
- creation window and dating basis;
- discovery location, discoverer, and chain of custody;
- access opportunities for the claimed author and possible forgers;
- substrate, ink, binding, insertion, erasure, repair, and aging findings;
- handwriting/diagram/phrase comparison features;
- device, archive, surveillance, genealogy, or institutional corroboration;
- competing explanations and confidence;
- what the evidence proves, suggests, or cannot establish;
- public/spoiler and privacy boundaries.

## Behavioral fingerprints

Potential features include letter formation, numeral style, spacing around punctuation, correction habits, arrows, tree layouts, abbreviations, repeated phrases, naming order, source-citation habits, pressure, and page planning. Use clusters, not one dramatic flourish.

Samuel may eventually imitate Konrad or distribute false Fitzgerald annotations. That is a proposed forensic opportunity, not an established detection mechanism. It must preserve plausible training, access, variation, and the possibility of deliberate disguise.

## Knowledge-state test

For each entry distinguish:

- what was true;
- what the writer knew;
- what the writer believed;
- what the writer wanted a future reader to believe;
- what later investigators infer;
- what the story ultimately authenticates.

This prevents journals from becoming omniscient exposition or retrospective proof without a causal access path.

A proposed evidence entry enters the Story Loop as a candidate scene or causal bridge.
