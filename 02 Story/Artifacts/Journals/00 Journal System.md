# Character Journal Artifact System

## Purpose

Make journals durable story objects rather than isolated image prompts. Every approved page must be traceable to a character, object, time, knowledge state, narrative purpose, visual profile, and evidence history.

A journal can simultaneously function as:

- private or performed character expression;
- a record whose omissions and distortions matter;
- a recurring visual artifact for public development;
- physical evidence with custody, dating, authorship, and tampering questions;
- a behavioral fingerprint that can connect documents across identities or generations.

## Four-layer identity

1. **Physical object:** dimensions, binding, cover, paper, instruments, numbering, inserts, damage, repairs, ownership marks, and era fit.
2. **Written hand:** slant, size, spacing, pressure, baseline, capitals, numerals, punctuation, corrections, abbreviations, and emotional variation.
3. **Visual thinking:** diagrams, maps, trees, arrows, drawings, marginalia, symbols, and layout habits.
4. **Narrative behavior:** what the character knows, believes, conceals, performs, revises, and leaves available as evidence.

## Authority rules

- A profile may be `proposed`, `working`, or `approved` as a visual design without changing story canon.
- Each entry separately labels story authority and artifact/image approval.
- No image becomes an approved journal reference until the character profile, physical design, handwriting state, knowledge state, approximate date, and narrative purpose are recorded.
- Approved face references establish identity only. They do not approve a journal, handwriting, page, prop, room, costume, or generated text.
- Generated lettering is illustrative until checked against the approved handwriting sheet and transcribed in the entry record.
- A beautiful page does not prove that the depicted event, wording, signature, material, or date is canon.
- Preserve ambiguity. A journal can be mistaken, forged, staged, altered, incomplete, or written for discovery.

## Production gate

Before generating a page, confirm:

1. entry ID and authority status;
2. object/version used at that date;
3. character knowledge and belief boundaries;
4. handwriting and diagram state;
5. exact visible text or a deliberate no-legible-text plan;
6. reference paths and approval levels;
7. evidence purpose and spoiler/public boundary;
8. missing definitions returned as `NEEDS DEFINITION` rather than invented.

After generation, record provenance, references, prompt, output path, transcription match, continuity findings, approval, and supersession.

## Moral and visual rule

Neat is not good and disorder is not evil. Journal design reveals cognition, pressure, performance, and change. Konrad may have beautiful handwriting; Samuel may make brilliant diagrams; Sylvan may create crowded pages because he refuses premature certainty; George's loss of imposed formatting may represent liberation.
