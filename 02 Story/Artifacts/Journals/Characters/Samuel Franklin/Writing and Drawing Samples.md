# Samuel Franklin — Writing and Drawing Samples

## Use and authority

This is a reusable style and transcription library, not a set of canonical journal entries. The short lines below are representative exercises consistent with Samuel's established character; they do not establish that he wrote them at any particular story date. Any generated lettering is illustrative until manually matched to these transcriptions.

## Hand construction

- Narrow semi-cursive with a 10–14° right slant, tall compressed ascenders, small counters, and tightly packed word spacing.
- Firm, even downstrokes; fast needle-like exits; baseline controlled until rage produces a slight upward drive.
- Capitals are spare and angular. `M`, `N`, and `K` are built from narrow verticals; `S` begins smaller than expected and finishes with a cutting lower curve.
- Lowercase `t` has a long late crossbar that can become an arrow. The `i` dot sits slightly ahead of the stem. Final `g` closes into a small hook.
- Numeral `1` has a top flag; `4` is open; `7` is crossed; `0` is narrow. Percentages and ratios are written more carefully than prose.
- Parentheses nest. Semicolons divide conditions. Em dashes introduce a concealed premise. Question marks are rare and usually tactical.
- Corrections preserve recoverability: one line through a word, replacement above, then a small branch to the consequence. When hiding intent, he blacks out selectively or pastes a new strip over the first wording.

## Emotional-state bands

### Controlled analyst

Spacing is tight but planned. One proposition per line. Underlines are straight and stop exactly beneath the key word. Diagrams remain separated from prose.

Sample: `Need: public vindication. Fear: visible dependence. Offer: a choice that conceals both.`

### Humiliated observer

Letter height stays controlled while pressure deepens. Quotes are copied with abnormally exact punctuation. Names receive double underlines; explanatory notes crowd the outer margin.

Sample: `He said “useful only at distance.” Preserve context. Preserve speaker. Preserve date.`

The quotation is a style exercise only; exact in-story wording remains unresolved.

### Obsessive replacement

Word spacing compresses, crossbars lengthen into routes, and formerly separate diagrams merge. Konrad-like capitals appear at headings. Samuel's own name is repeatedly placed at the convergence point.

Sample: `Not revenge—continuity.` The dash is overwritten twice and the second word is boxed.

### Exposure pressure

Still intelligent and legible. Multiple inks reveal time-separated interventions; arrows reverse direction; fallback scripts are inserted between older lines. The page becomes evidential because it preserves the order of maintenance.

Sample: `If questioned: deny access / concede observation / attribute action to George / reopen channel.`

## Drawing sample library

1. **Pressure-point portrait:** a name in a narrow rectangle, with four outward spokes labeled posture, private need, feared witness, and acceptable self-explanation.
2. **Lie tree:** one true trunk fact divides into audience-specific branches; dotted cross-links mark details that must remain consistent.
3. **Conversation funnel:** three plausible openings narrow toward one apparent voluntary choice.
4. **Reaction clock:** concentric arcs estimate immediate, delayed, and witnessed responses.
5. **Dependency circuit:** resources and permissions flow in one direction while gratitude and perceived authorship flow backward.
6. **Replacement ladder:** office, symbol, access, archive, family, and memory arranged as successive footholds.
7. **Occupied genealogy:** an inherited tree redrawn so unrelated branches pass through a single controlling node.
8. **Contradiction braid:** incompatible stories are kept apart until a shared verified fact lets them appear mutually supporting.
9. **Isolation chamber:** a person is drawn inside nested information boundaries with selected inputs and staged confirmations.
10. **Intervention ledger:** each covert correction is a short horizontal stroke crossing a dated vertical line.
11. **Name substitution study:** the same role box is relabeled in three hands to test visual legitimacy.
12. **Failure recursion:** a corrective action points back into the condition that made correction necessary.

## Unique writing–drawing combinations

### The sentence becomes the mechanism

A key noun is underlined; its crossbar extends into a diagram node; the diagram returns to a rewritten verb in the same sentence. Samuel's prose does not caption the drawing—the prose is physically converted into it.

### The margin invades the claim

The main text states the official interpretation. A dense outer-margin model predicts each reader's response, then arrows cross inward and replace words in the official line. The page visibly shows him editing what others will remember.

### The occupied name

Konrad's name begins as the heading. Samuel's annotations surround it, lines pass through it, and a later capital style mimics it. The compositional center shifts without the original heading being removed.

### Prediction/observation split

Left column: exact predicted responses. Right column: observed behavior. Center seam: small deviations and the next manipulation. This is the most important way to show that Samuel is genuinely brilliant before obsession distorts him.

## Forensic fingerprint cluster

Use at least five features before proposing common authorship: crossed `7`; forward `i` dot; long late `t` bar; semicolon-separated conditions; narrow name boxes; arrowheads made with two unequal strokes; single-line recoverable corrections; ratios written with unusual care; conclusion inserted above rather than rewritten below; branch labels ordered by audience rather than chronology.

Deliberate imitation can copy capitals and spacing but should not automatically reproduce timing, pressure, correction order, diagram logic, or the forward `i` dot.
