# Participant Governance and Command Rules

## Core rule

Participants on the colonization planet are governed by **very strict rules** even when they are given access to extremely powerful technological systems.

The colony's command-and-control architecture may be capable of reaching effectively all technology on the planet, but participant authority is never equivalent to unrestricted system ownership.

The key distinction is:

**Universal technical reach does not imply universal participant permission.**

Participants operate inside a rule-bound environment designed to preserve agency, accountability, evidence, containment integrity, and the developmental purpose of the colony.

## Participant authority model

Participant access should be constrained by combinations of:

- identity;
- assigned role;
- current stage of the environment;
- physical location;
- task or declared purpose;
- time window;
- system category;
- authorization tier;
- local confirmation;
- safety interlocks;
- quorum or independent approval;
- immutable logging;
- automatic review or escalation triggers.

The exact matrix remains unresolved and should be developed only as story requirements demand.

## What strict rules accomplish

The rules prevent a powerful participant from simply converting the colony into arbitrary reality control.

They also create the possibility for adversarial ingenuity: Samuel, George, or a Daemon can become dangerous by discovering how to use legitimate permissions, ambiguities, timing windows, delegated authority, institutional habits, and interacting subsystems in ways the designers did not intend.

This means the strongest antagonistic behavior often resembles exploit development rather than godlike command:

`understand the rule -> discover the boundary -> combine permitted actions -> produce an unintended effect -> remain technically inside or near the rule until challenged`

The colony can therefore be extraordinarily powerful while still generating meaningful conflict.

## Rule-bound hostile control

When Samuel and George gain greater control after the exploratory stage, their hostile phase should not remove the participant rules.

Instead, they acquire enough legitimate or delegated authority to make the rules dangerous.

Their Daemon can search continuously for ways to weaponize:

- permitted surveillance;
- routing;
- environmental adaptation;
- synthetic tasking;
- information delivery;
- access control;
- scheduling and timing;
- social coordination;
- public interpretation;
- institutional process;
- combinations of individually lawful commands.

The horror comes from orchestration within constraints.

## Important narrative consequence

Strict rules mean Samuel cannot simply command: "destroy Sylvan."

He has to produce conditions under which Sylvan breaks, fails, becomes disqualified, or reaches whatever processing threshold the colony recognizes.

That makes the humiliation stage a genuine contest rather than an execution.

Samuel's problem is therefore not lack of technological reach. It is that the system still contains rules he cannot openly violate without losing legitimacy, triggering intervention, creating evidence, or invalidating the process.

Sylvan's survival depends partly on learning those boundaries faster than Samuel can exploit them.

## Relationship to Luminai and Daemons

A healthy Luminai can help a participant understand what is authorized, preserve provenance, detect conflicts between commands and rules, and distinguish technical capability from legitimate action.

A Daemon seeks the opposite advantage: every permission becomes an attack surface, every exception a possible exploit, every ambiguity a place to push the system toward its obsession.

The difference is not that one has technology and the other does not. They can touch the same command fabric. Their relationship to constraint is different.

## Hard constraints

- Participants remain accountable for actions carried out through their Luminai or Daemon.
- A participant cannot erase the existence of the governing rules merely by gaining higher access.
- High-level commands should leave provenance unless a specific story mechanism establishes otherwise.
- Some systems must retain independent refusal, review, or evidence functions.
- The environment must preserve a path by which abusive use of legitimate authority can later be authenticated.
- Strict rules cannot become arbitrary authorial barriers introduced only when needed to save Sylvan; important limits must be established before payoff.
- Samuel and George may exploit rules, but they cannot silently rewrite the entire constitutional logic of the colony.

## Open questions

1. What categories of participant rules exist across all stages of the colony?
2. Which rules are technical enforcement and which are legal/institutional requirements?
3. What authority changes when Samuel and George gain control at the end of the exploratory stage?
4. Which actions remain categorically prohibited even then?
5. What constitutes a violation serious enough to remove or process a participant?
6. What must Samuel accomplish to cause Sylvan to be processed without openly invalidating the contest?
7. Which logs or independent systems preserve evidence of rule exploitation?
8. What happens when a participant follows the literal rule while clearly violating its intended purpose?
9. Can a Daemon combine many individually permitted actions into an outcome no single rule anticipated?
10. Which participant rules ultimately make Samuel's eventual defeat unavoidable if Sylvan survives long enough?

## Story-development principle

When designing any powerful ability for Samuel, George, Sylvan, a Luminai, or a Daemon, define four things at the same time:

1. what the system can technically do;
2. what the participant is permitted to do;
3. what evidence the action leaves;
4. what happens if the participant abuses the permission.

This should keep advanced-environment scenes powerful, legible, and strategically interesting rather than arbitrary.
