# Identities and Recurrence

Contained people may serve sentences through complete public lives: new names, biographies, professions, families, and admired roles. The public biography is not necessarily a cover story for hidden activity; it may be the containment role itself.

Recurring individuals are detected through behavior rather than faces: unusual technical solutions, linguistic habits, negotiation patterns, handwriting, artistic motifs, architecture, or decision signatures. The archive can therefore be correct about the admirable public life and wrong about who was living it.

Open: memory across identities, authorization of recasting, synthetic participation, exposure consequences, and the specific behavioral signature that begins the story.
