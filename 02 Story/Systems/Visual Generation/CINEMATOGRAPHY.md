# Cinematography

## Start with the dramatic instant

Choose the fraction of action that reveals change: the hand leaving the control, the glance after recognition, the room ceasing to obey, the person entering before anyone has turned, or the breath between accusation and response. Avoid completed poses that make characters look arranged for a poster.

## Camera choices

- **Wide:** relationship between people, institutions, and environment.
- **Medium:** behavior, hands, status, and interaction.
- **Close:** recognition, concealed reaction, bodily cost, or evidence detail.
- **High or low angle:** use for a specific spatial or psychological relation, not automatic power symbolism.
- **Off-axis camera:** creates observed reality and reduces rigid symmetry.
- **Foreground obstruction:** suggests a larger physical world and creates depth when motivated.
- **Lens feel:** describe spatial effect—compressed distance, intimate proximity, broad context—rather than using numbers alone.

## Blocking and behavior

Give each subject a task. State foot direction, weight, hand use, gaze target, interpersonal distance, and whether the person is arriving, leaving, resisting, concealing, or changing course. Emotional direction should be visible through behavior rather than labels alone.

## Light

Use motivated sources: dawn through structure, practical task lighting, emergency systems, reflected city light, fire, moon infrastructure, or filtered atmosphere. Define what the light reveals and what remains difficult to read. Preserve the vault's color grammar as meaning, not as a filter spread equally over every surface.

## Natural variation

Across a sequence, vary:

- shot size and camera side;
- subject position and depth;
- action phase and expression;
- wardrobe appropriate to time and role;
- weather, atmosphere, and practical light;
- foreground, negative space, and environmental evidence.

Do not vary immutable identity merely to make images look different.

## Anti-rigidity pass

If an output looks like the reference photograph with a new background:

1. change the camera relation and crop;
2. give the subject an incomplete physical action;
3. replace wardrobe through era and task logic;
4. change the motivated light direction;
5. add foreground or relational blocking;
6. state explicitly which reference colors and composition must not be copied.

The goal is not random novelty. It is the same person photographed in another real moment.
