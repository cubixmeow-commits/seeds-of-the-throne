# Character Identity Locks

## Purpose

An identity lock answers **who remains the same** while allowing the photograph to become new. It protects stable human geometry and continuity without freezing pose, wardrobe, palette, or emotional state.

## Identity hierarchy

1. **Primary identity master** — the best approved face and body anchor.
2. **Required angle references** — straight-on, strict profile, and front three-quarter when available.
3. **Supporting references** — approved age, expression, body, wardrobe, or relational evidence.
4. **Scene candidates** — useful interpretations that cannot redefine identity.

Follow `skills/create-seeds-images/references/visual-registry.json` for current approved files and reference priority.

## Immutable packet

Record only traits that should survive almost every scene:

- facial geometry and silhouette;
- apparent age range;
- stable hairline, hair texture, and facial hair logic;
- eye placement, nose, mouth, jaw, ears, and distinguishing marks;
- body proportions, height relationship, and habitual physical weight;
- traits explicitly established by story canon.

Avoid poetic descriptors that models cannot test. Prefer relational geometry: narrower than, deep-set, high bridge, broad lower face, asymmetric brow, or compact build.

## Flexible packet

Allow scene logic to determine:

- clothing and grooming within era and role;
- expression and emotional visibility;
- pose, gesture, gaze, and hand use;
- camera side, lens, crop, and distance;
- palette, lighting, weather, and location;
- age variation when the timeline requires it;
- injury, fatigue, status, and environmental effects.

## Multi-character scenes

Lock each character separately before describing the relationship. State relative height, distance, eyelines, touch, and who occupies foreground or background. Prevent face blending by giving each subject a distinct action and spatial position. Do not ask a model to infer two identities from one paired poster when separate identity masters exist.

## Luminai presentation

When a Luminai receives a face, voice, avatar, or projected body, specify that it is differentiated presentation of the human's extended cognition, not a separate romantic companion. Apply the developing gender-presentation rule only when the character's orientation and chosen presentation are known. Do not infer orientation from appearance.

## Drift checklist

- Does facial width remain stable across camera angles?
- Did apparent age change accidentally?
- Did hair, beard, skin detail, or body proportions drift toward a generic archetype?
- Did two characters blend traits?
- Did the model preserve reference clothing or pose without scene reason?
- Did a symbolic visual detail become accidental story canon?

Reject identity drift before polishing style. A beautiful wrong person is not a continuity success.
