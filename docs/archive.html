Story reality / development reality

Every event can leave three different records.

Seeds of the Throne is both a story about controlled interpretation and a public experiment in story development. This archive makes both layers legible: what happened inside the world, what people were told happened, what evidence survived, and how firmly the current story treats each idea.

Reader promise

Working directions are not presented as finished canon. Unresolved choices are not silently filled. Historical notes can conflict until the author deliberately decides between them.

In-world evidence model

One event. Three records.

Select a layer to see how Samuel’s False Victory survives ordinary verification, and what Sylvan’s archive must compare to defeat it.

● What actually occurs

George’s apparent success ends in real containment.

Samuel and George privately commit serious wrongdoing and attempt a horribly planned takeover of the largest government. The plan collapses in a humiliating exposure, and George is busted into containment. Konrad’s later conquest regime also loses its state, empire, and war.

The event
Real apparent success, failed takeover, exposure, and containment.
The meaning
George is not the victor’s secret ruler.

● What the faction is told

George secretly captured the victor from within.

Samuel interprets the same visible facts as proof that defeat was planned. Public office becomes covert command; restriction becomes secrecy; failure becomes hidden strategy.

The event
George really holds prestigious roles.
The lie
Those roles prove the faction won.

◐ What Sylvan can compare

George failed publicly. Samuel later rewrote the humiliation as infiltration.

Records of apparent success, private conduct, the failed takeover, George’s bust, Samuel’s later explanation, wartime sabotage, and continuing command decisions reveal when failure was converted into victory mythology.

Proof structure
Event → original record → later revision → beneficiary → next operation.
Ethical constraint
Prove the system without indiscriminately exposing descendants.

Development evidence model

How settled is each part?

Status is part of the content. It tells visitors what the author has decided and where the public is watching ideas become story.

The macro premise

One colonization world cultivates legitimate heirs and contains dangerous leaders.

● Established

The Great War

Konrad’s faction attempts world conquest while exiled Samuel sabotages it from inside the future victor; defeat brings the leadership into Samuel’s containment environment.

● Established

The endgame revelation

Sylvan discovers Samuel, not George, is the hidden decision-maker maintaining the conflict.

◐ Established core / unresolved specifics

Samuel’s misconduct and exile

The underage relationship and exile are established. Additional abuses, exact circumstances, parental roles, and Samuel’s path into containment remain open; no child from that relationship is established.

● Established direction

Samuel’s earlier betrayal

He performs reconciliation with his parents while covertly sabotaging Konrad’s campaign, then conceals that betrayal when the defeated faction enters his environment.

● Established role / working viewpoint

The protagonist architecture

Sylvan carries the modern Inheritor role and is the leading primary protagonist. One of Orzai’s parents is the leading Great War Witness candidate. Whether that earlier thread receives substantial viewpoint remains open.

● Established hinge / unresolved mechanism

The containment migration

Konrad moves or reactivates Fitzgerald machinery inside Samuel’s bounded jurisdiction after defeat. Samuel’s earlier first-primary priority matters; the exact hierarchy, lock, permissions, evidence, and reset limits do not yet exist.

◐ Accepted direction

The modern environment ladder

Sylvan moves from unemployment and Luminai emergence through antagonist-dominant humiliation toward multi-zone employment and bounded control. Locations, costs, transitions, and end mechanism remain open.

The edge of the current story

Questions that change the novel.

These are not minor lore gaps. Each controls plot, agency, evidence, or emotional consequence.

  1. Hierarchy

    What is the containment hierarchy in practical terms, and what does first-primary priority actually control?

  2. Luminai

    What interfaces, risks, costs, and unprecedented capability emerge from Sylvan’s reciprocal development?

  3. Environment

    What are the locations, permissions, transitions, survival clock, evidence path, and bounded terminal mechanism?

  4. Samuel

    What exact lie induces the post-defeat migration, and which first record reveals that access runs backward?

  5. George

    What is the first anomaly showing that someone is continuously writing around his reality?

  6. Relationship

    How do Sylvan and Orzai synchronize selected context while preserving privacy, consent, repair, and separate agency?

  7. Witness

    Which of Orzai’s parents carries the Great War viewpoint, and what survives into Sylvan’s story?

  8. Ending

    What does George choose, what does Sylvan lose, and how does Samuel’s replacement-heir contingency persist?

Build-in-public method

Develop with AI. Write the fiction by hand.

The vault is now explicitly development-first. Finished-novel prose is downstream from scoped retrieval, gap analysis, alternatives, selective research, multiscale structure, and author selection.

The active story loop

  • scope one target and classify authority
  • generate breadth cheaply before expanding depth
  • research only mechanisms that can change the story
  • run the Multiscale Development Gauntlet when a gap crosses era, arc, sequence, chapter, and scene
  • use only relevant critics
  • stop at the author gate before promotion

Prototype and prose boundary

  • route divergent non-canon generation through the separate Story Exploration Lab
  • 500 to 1,500 word prototypes are disposable and non-canon
  • clear objectives and visible state change come first
  • Archive Thriller remains a later manuscript candidate
  • final fiction requires the author’s voice and judgment
  • persuasive output never outranks an explicit decision
