Blocking · Opening + viewpoint

Choose who carries the story.

This session should turn the project’s strongest endgame material into a usable beginning. Work in order: make the viewpoint decision first, define Sylvan around a personal want and cost, then design the event that forces the story to begin. Keep answers provisional but specific. Do not expand the world unless a story decision requires it. Finish by recording what became canon, what remains working, and what should be revisited.

Session agenda

  1. 01
    Choose the protagonist architecture

    Decide whether Sylvan is the opening narrator and whether the Witness, Inheritor, and protagonist are one role or several.

  2. 02
    Build Sylvan as a person

    Give him a pre-archive desire, a formative relationship, a flaw, and a personal cost for preserving the truth.

  3. 03
    Design the opening disturbance

    Choose the event that breaks normal life, creates an immediate loss, and makes waiting impossible.

  4. 04
    Trace the evidence chain

    Define why Sylvan records, what proof survives, and how it can eventually reach George without feeling convenient.

  5. 05
    Close with decisions

    Write five settled statements, label provisional ideas, and park questions that do not block the opening.

Questions that unlock the draft

Answer these before moving on

  • Who experiences the first scene, and why is that person the best lens?
  • Is Sylvan the protagonist, the witness, the inheritor—or a bridge between those roles?
  • What does Sylvan want before the archive becomes his burden?
  • Which relationship makes his evidence emotionally meaningful rather than merely informative?
  • What flaw makes preserving or revealing the truth difficult for him?
  • What event forces the story to begin now instead of a year earlier or later?
  • What does Sylvan lose in the opening that cannot simply be restored?
  • What does he risk by showing George the truth instead of only defeating him?

Narrative pressure

The central dramatic machine

The father’s strategy turns biography into infrastructure: every role prepares George to misunderstand one final choice.

  1. 01
    False Victory

    The contained faction mistakes continued status and access for proof that it won.

  2. 02
    Counterfeit succession

    George’s public roles create the appearance that he is approaching genuine inheritance.

  3. 03
    Shared distortion

    George and the Daemon reinforce a threat model built from compromised evidence.

  4. 04
    The archive survives

    Sylvan preserves evidence the father cannot safely allow his own faction to see.

  5. 05
    The father loses

    Sylvan wins the legitimacy struggle before George understands that the war has changed.

  6. 06
    The final substitution

    George is sent forward anyway—as weapon, distraction, and intended scapegoat.

  7. 07
    The first informed choice

    The evidence reaches George while he is already in position to fulfill the design.

World logic

The world beneath the story

The technology works best as a layered civilization, not one omnipotent intelligence.

Layer 1

Parent civilization

Long-lived humans build worlds, assign roles, cultivate heirs, and govern the process from beyond ordinary awareness.

Layer 2

Influence and authorization

Competing plans are weighted through identity, standing, domain, delegation, risk, and system constraints.

Layer 3

Lumina and Daemon

Lifelong agents remember, interpret, negotiate, and represent their humans inside systems those humans cannot directly see.

Layer 4

Hidden planetary infrastructure

Sensing, communications, computation, energy, and limited actuation operate beneath the visible historical layer.

Layer 5

Constructed civilization

Biological synthetics build cultures and institutions experienced by ordinary inhabitants as the whole of reality.

01

Containment is biography

A sentence may be an admired public life rather than a cell.

02

Influence is indirect

People experience cooperation, friction, resources, and delay—not a visible score.

03

Awakening is partial

Every explanation is filtered through era, role, and authorization.

04

Power remains constrained

Capability does not equal authority, and no actor controls the complete stack.

Creative trajectory

Recommended decision sequence

Each decision unlocks the next. The order keeps worldbuilding subordinate to the story’s human movement.

  1. 1

    Choose the protagonist architecture

    Decide how Sylvan relates to the Witness, Inheritor, and tested first-person narrator.

    Next
  2. 2

    Define Sylvan before adding systems

    Give him a prior desire, authority, formative relationship, flaw, reason to record, and personal cost.

    Queued
  3. 3

    Choose the archive’s first decisive proof

    One comprehensible abuse, one consequence, one authentication method, and one denial it defeats.

    Queued
  4. 4

    Build the opening-to-archive chain

    Inciting contradiction, recurrence clue, first ally, retaliation, and point of no return.

    Queued
  5. 5

    Specify the confrontation’s constraints

    What each side can do, what requires authorization, which audience matters, and what changes publicly.

    Queued
  6. 6

    Choose George’s action and Sylvan’s cost

    Lock the first informed choice and the price that keeps disclosure from becoming an easy victory.

    Queued
  7. 7

    Design the aftermath through the other son

    Show how a regime can lose without its networks, bargains, and methods disappearing at once.

    Queued
