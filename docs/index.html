Science Fiction · Conspiracy · Generational Power

A world built to contain monsters. A throne built on the belief that no one would ever uncover why.

Seeds of the Throne follows a struggle across generations, identities, and engineered worlds as buried crimes become inherited power. At its center are men who learned to turn people into instruments, descendants raised inside plans they never understood, and the people forced to prove that a manufactured history can still leave evidence behind.

The story in three movements.

The vault is large, but the central conflict is not complicated: power is seized, hidden inside a system meant to outlast its architects, and eventually challenged by people who were never supposed to see the whole design.

  1. 01
    A world is built with two purposes

    A reconstructed world becomes both a place of cultivation and a containment environment. The people inside it inherit lives, loyalties, and histories without necessarily knowing who designed them.

The pressure beneath the story

Every secret creates a debt.

The central characters can lie about who they are, rewrite public history, manipulate descendants, and hide behind engineered systems. What they cannot control forever is the chain of consequences those choices leave behind.

The central promiseThe truth is buried, not erased.The deeper the conspiracy reaches, the more dangerous its surviving evidence becomes.
01 · Responsibility

Who actually gave the orders?

Power means little without accountability. The story follows the trail from public roles and plausible deniability back to the people who designed the system.

02 · Initiating loss

What does Sylvan lose first?

The modern conflict begins when an old conspiracy stops being history and becomes personal, irreversible, and impossible for Sylvan to ignore.

03 · Luminai limits

What happens when the mind extends beyond itself?

Luminai can deepen perception and agency, but power without limits would destroy the stakes. Their capabilities matter because their limits matter more.

04 · Evidence chain

Can the truth survive its enemies?

Records are created, hidden, stolen, authenticated, doubted, and preserved. The final struggle depends on making the past impossible to dismiss.

05 · Victory cost

What is exposure worth?

Winning cannot mean becoming another version of the people who built the throne. The final victory has to cost something and reveal what Sylvan refuses to become.

Why the conflict works

The villains are not waiting to be defeated. They are building futures.

The strongest part of the story is the way personal obsession, institutional power, family, and long-term planning become the same weapon. The conspiracy survives because it is designed to continue through other people.

● Central conflict

Samuel and Konrad

Both men are brilliant manipulators who believe they can turn every relationship into leverage. Their greatest strength becomes their weakness: each builds systems so dependent on control that eventually those systems begin producing evidence against them.
