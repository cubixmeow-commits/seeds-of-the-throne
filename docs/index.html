The complete story system

An interplanetary civilization built its most ambitious world to grow leaders and contain tyrants.

Humanity already thrives on numerous planets under leaders who have lived for thousands of years. Their earlier successes make this uniquely advanced, technologically managed world possible. Heirs and contained criminals enter the same constructed history, and across a century of war, hidden systems, engineered bloodlines, and competing realities, those two projects collide.

The argument underneath the plot

What may one generation do with the next?

The same question produces three incompatible ideas of inheritance, development, and control.

Konrad Fitzgerald

Continuity without succession

Descendants inherit identity, doctrine, status, and obligation. The patriarchs retain permanent authority. Children extend the founders’ power but must never become independent enough to replace them.

Inheritance preserves the hierarchy.
Samuel Franklin

Inheritance as weapon

A failed privileged heir captures other people’s lineage systems. If his inheritance was denied or exposed as undeserved, he will corrupt everyone else’s certainty about ancestry and succession.

If mine was denied, yours can be destroyed.
The real leaders

Preparation for independence

Beloved children receive difficult, consequential lives so they can form judgment, peers, happiness, and legitimacy beyond inherited office. Their parents hope to be surpassed.

Power creates obligations to the future.
Sylvan Elaria

People remain ends

Sylvan must expose a system built from instrumentalized children without turning George, the other son, or private descendants into instruments of his own victory.

Truth does not excuse using people as proof.
