Contained criminal leadership

They talk about inheritance.
What they actually want is permanent control.

Inheritance, bloodline purity, controlled succession, and generational submission belong primarily to the criminal faction. Their younger generations are not being prepared to replace them. They are being produced to extend the senior generation’s power.

Konrad Fitzgerald

Continuity without succession

Konrad wants his family line, doctrine, institutions, and hierarchy to continue indefinitely while he and the senior generation remain at the top. Descendants inherit identity, duty, status, and obligation, but not ultimate authority. Prestigious younger figures remain subordinate to the patriarchs.

Samuel Franklin

Inheritance as grievance and weapon

Samuel is a failed privileged heir whom the faction mistakes for an ideological ally. He is increasingly driven by jealousy, sabotage, acquisition, and the desire to corrupt the genealogical certainty and succession systems that others treat as sacred.

George White

The Throwaway Prince

George belongs to the descendant generation placed directly into the reconstructed world. He believes he is being prepared to inherit, while his father’s system increasingly reveals that children are instruments, contingencies, and disposable extensions of senior power.
