# BA-08: George's final choice

## Purpose

Give George agency in the climax rather than using him only as Samuel's operational scapegoat.

## Established

Samuel drags George into the apocalyptic living-god attempt and positions him to absorb operational blame. George is a direct participant on the newer planet and retains an independent evidence route he has repeatedly failed to use.

Sources: [[02 Story/Characters/George White - The Collapse of the Lie]], [[02 Story/Characters/George White - A Life Beneath the Ice]], [[07 Coordination/Story Completion Workflow/Reassessment Workshop/07 - George and Konrad functions]].

## Four possibilities

1. **Late corroboration:** George authenticates a crucial record after realizing Samuel intends to abandon him.
2. **Operational refusal:** George refuses one final action, limiting harm without erasing his earlier participation.
3. **Self-protective betrayal:** George exposes Samuel primarily to save himself, producing useful truth without moral absolution.
4. **Failed escape with evidence:** George tries to preserve both loyalty and innocence, fails, but leaves a record that becomes decisive.

## Central author gate

What final action does Samuel require from George, when does George understand the scapegoat design, what does George choose, and how does that choice affect harm, evidence, and his own accountability?

## Scene test

George's decision changes at least one outcome but does not erase the consequences of his previous choices.

## Adversarial test

If George becomes heroic through one late act, accountability becomes sentimental. If nothing he does matters, decades of direct participation have no dramatic payoff.
