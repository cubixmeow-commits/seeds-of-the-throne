# Seeds of the Throne: Surface Archive

## Core idea

Surface Archive turns authentic wildlife and environmental photography into accessible cultural records inspired by life on the *Seeds of the Throne* colonization planet.

The photographs show the constructive result of colonization. An engineered environment becomes a genuine home. Wildlife, weather, shorelines, plants, and ordinary routines gain meaning because they are alive, not because they are secretly controlled or placed as symbols.

## Governing tone

The default tone is:

- positive;
- affectionate;
- observant;
- lightly funny;
- curious;
- life affirming;
- simple enough to enjoy without knowing the story.

The archive should create wonder before suspicion. Criminal justice, containment, prosecution, exposure, surveillance, and adversarial evidence are not default wildlife framing.

## Identity hierarchy

Use this order:

1. **Seeds of the Throne** as the parent story.
2. **Surface Archive** as the photography series.
3. A record number and descriptive category.
4. A short image-specific observation or joke.
5. An optional Luminai note that adds a second, constructive interpretation.

Example:

```text
SEEDS OF THE THRONE
SURFACE ARCHIVE
RECORD 0043 · SHORELINE OBSERVATION

THE REPORT DESCRIBED THE BEACH AS UNOCCUPIED.
THE RESIDENT DECLINED TO COMMENT.

LUMINAI NOTE: ABSENCE OF AUTHORITY IS NOT ABSENCE OF LIFE.
```

## Visual system

The photograph controls the composition. Preserve the real animal, count, pose, anatomy, behavior, habitat, focus, depth of field, and natural light.

Use the current Seeds X banner as the complementary visual family:

| Element | Surface Archive use |
| --- | --- |
| Deep navy | Sparse edge gradients and text support |
| Parchment or cream | Primary readable typography |
| Antique gold | Series titles, borders, and archive rules |
| System blue | Fine cartographic, orbital, ecological, or connection lines |
| Coral or ember red | Two or three small registration or authentication points |
| Black and charcoal | High-contrast framing when the source supports it |

Do not impose the banner's book, desk, cities, or collage on each photograph. Translate its palette and information language instead.

## Composition rules

- Keep at least 75 to 80 percent of the photograph visually dominant.
- Use existing negative space for titles and archive information.
- Keep graphic geometry at the edges or in naturally empty areas.
- Do not cover the animal with text or interface marks.
- Preserve the source aspect ratio unless a deliberate crop improves the photograph without removing meaningful habitat.
- Keep all essential copy readable in an X feed preview on a phone.
- Avoid dense dossiers, fake coordinates, generic science-fiction interfaces, and decorative clutter.
- Never add glowing animals, fantasy anatomy, spaceships, visible hidden infrastructure, or unsupported environmental details.

## Story relationship

Surface Archive is story-inspired public material. It may represent the kinds of wildlife records, cultural images, or observations that could exist within the surface civilization, but an individual image does not establish a species, location, event, technology, or historical fact as canon unless the author separately accepts it into story memory.

The archive's recurring ideas should emphasize:

- engineered land becoming a living place;
- cooperation that preserves independence;
- attention to small lives;
- growth and adaptation;
- ordinary existence having value beyond powerful people's plans;
- shared reality without uniform interpretation;
- the surface civilization being worth protecting for its own sake.

## Record categories

- Coastal Observation
- Shoreline Observation
- Wildlife Record
- Habitat Record
- Seasonal Record
- Field Record
- Ordinary Life
- Before Disclosure
- Luminai Observation
- Recovered Photograph

These are flexible labels, not separate canon systems.

## X writing direction

Public posts should normally contain one to three short paragraphs. Begin with the photograph's place inside the larger story idea, then connect the visible life to a constructive theme. Avoid explaining the entire colonization system.

Recommended pattern:

```text
In Seeds of the Throne, [ordinary natural moment] is part of [larger constructive idea].

[One or two clear sentences connecting the real photograph to life, home, growth, cooperation, independence, adaptation, or shared reality.]
```

No em dashes. Hashtags, coordinates, capture dates, and technical archive language are optional rather than mandatory.

## Approval boundary

The identity, tone, and visual direction in this note are author-approved. Individual photographs, generated layouts, captions, locations, and record classifications still require episode-level review.
