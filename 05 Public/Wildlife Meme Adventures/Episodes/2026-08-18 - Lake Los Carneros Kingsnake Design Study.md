## Selected design process

1. **Lock factual truth.** Preserve one real snake, its black-and-cream markings, extended forked tongue, dry grass, soil, and the author-supplied capture details.
2. **Extract the visual vocabulary.** Use charcoal and parchment from the animal's banding, dusty olive and warm earth from the habitat, and terracotta as a restrained echo of the tongue.
3. **Translate the visible action into a premise.** The extended tongue becomes a harmless field-survey tool, producing `THE TONGUE SURVEY`, `ONE QUICK SITE INSPECTION`, and `FINDINGS: EXTREMELY SNAKEABLE.`
4. **Explore structurally different directions.** Test field-journal, trail-survey, and cinematic dusk approaches rather than recoloring one layout.
5. **Select for photograph-design integration.** The trail-survey direction best connects the dry terrain, the snake's movement, and the joke through torn map paper, contour lines, route marks, and field-check icons.
6. **Correct the reading order.** Revision 2 uses a simple top-to-bottom sequence: identity and title, photographic encounter, then comic findings. Each function has its own band.
7. **Protect the photograph.** Keep contours and icons in the graphic panels rather than laying them across the animal. Make the snake the uninterrupted center of the poster.
8. **Preserve iteration history.** Retain the original concepts and first selected draft so later episodes can learn from the choice without repeating its premise or exact treatment.

### Fixed versus adaptive elements

- Fixed: small `WILDLIFE MEME ADVENTURES` identity, `LOCAL WILDLIFE PHOTOGRAPHY`, authentic source subject, factual boundaries, phone-size legibility, and X-ready portrait format.
- Adaptive: palette, display type, paper treatment, panel proportions, graphic motifs, episode title, joke, and payoff.
- Specific to this episode: torn trail-map paper, contour lines, search/check icons, dusty olive findings panel, tongue-survey premise, and the three-band order.
- Reuse rule: the process may recur; the tongue-survey joke and exact art treatment should not recur soon.
