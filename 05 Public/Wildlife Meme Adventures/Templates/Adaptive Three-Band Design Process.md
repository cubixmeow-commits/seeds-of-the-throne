# Adaptive Three-Band Design Process

Use this process when a wildlife photograph benefits from a strong editorial reading order and a dedicated photographic center. It records the successful revision process from `The Tongue Survey`; it is a compositional option, not a mandatory series layout.

## Canvas decision comes first

Inspect the source photograph's dimensions, orientation, aspect ratio, composition, and protected details before choosing this process. Preserve a strong source canvas by default. Do not select three bands merely because a previous episode succeeded with them, and do not force a landscape or square photograph into portrait proportions to make the bands familiar.

If three bands fit the image, adapt their direction and proportions to the selected canvas. A landscape photograph may support shallow horizontal information bands around a dominant panorama; another image may not support bands at all. The retained series reference supplies visual language, not fixed dimensions.

## Composition

1. **Top — identity and premise.** Use a high-contrast graphic panel for the small umbrella identity and large original episode title. When the title appears in a ribbon, the ribbon must be sized for immediate readability in the native-aspect feed preview, not merely in the production master.
2. **Middle — authentic encounter.** Give the real photograph an uninterrupted full-width band. Keep the animal, visible behavior, habitat, and important negative space intact.
3. **Bottom — comic resolution.** Use a contrasting graphic panel for one setup line and one payoff. Keep both readable without competing with the title.

Irregular transitions may connect the panels when their material language suits the image. Graphic marks belong primarily in the top and bottom panels, not over the animal.

## Image-responsive theming

- Sample two structural neutrals from the subject.
- Sample one supporting color from the habitat.
- Add no more than one restrained accent tied to a visible detail.
- Check that title, photograph, and payoff remain distinct at feed size.
- Keep ribbon lettering substantially larger than supporting identity text. Widen the ribbon, reduce ornaments, or use a deliberate two-line title before shrinking the type.
- Let the photograph suggest the material metaphor: map, journal, archive card, weather report, field notice, or another original device.
- Do not force the same palette or panel proportions onto the next photograph.

## Concept exploration

Before selecting a direction, make candidate concepts different in premise and structure—not merely color variants. For each candidate define:

- title;
- setup and payoff;
- palette source;
- material or graphic metaphor;
- photograph crop and protected details;
- reason the treatment belongs to this photograph.

## Selection test

Prefer the direction that:

- makes the real photograph the strongest evidence;
- respects the source photograph's composition and chosen aspect ratio rather than imposing a familiar canvas;
- creates an immediate top-to-bottom reading order;
- derives its colors and motifs from visible details;
- supports the joke without presenting it as wildlife fact;
- remains recognizable as Wildlife Meme Adventures with restrained branding;
- keeps any episode ribbon immediately readable at ordinary feed size without zooming;
- introduces no false anatomy, habitat, species, location, or behavior claims.

## Archive requirements

Keep the source photograph, all materially different concepts, the selected revision, exact prompts or generation brief, checksums, author feedback, factual uncertainties, caption status, and publication status. Selecting an image does not authorize publication.

## Origin lesson

In `The Tongue Survey`, moving the cream title panel above the photograph and keeping the olive findings panel below it clarified the complete story in one glance: premise, encounter, result. The process is reusable; the snake-specific trail survey treatment is not a default.
