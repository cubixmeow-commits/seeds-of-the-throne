# Wildlife Meme Adventures

This is the governing note for creating **Wildlife Meme Adventures** posts for X from the author's local wildlife photographs. Read this note before designing a new episode.

## Core identity

- Make **Wildlife Meme Adventures** clearly readable and prominent, but keep its scale restrained and responsive to the photograph. It must never dominate the animal or consume excessive image area.
- Keep **Local Wildlife Photography** as the factual photography label.
- The authentic photograph is the hero. Branding and jokes support it rather than replacing it.
- Every photograph receives a completely original episode title, joke, and visual direction.
- A prior post is a reference for the flexible brand language, not a fixed layout to copy.

## Tone and humor

- Default to warm, friendly, playful humor that makes the animal or moment feel charming.
- Prefer jokes about snacks, curiosity, personality, awkwardness, weather, movement, scenery, or ordinary animal behavior.
- Do not use military, tactical, enforcement, combat, ownership, territorial, threat, patrol, perimeter, security, or conflict framing unless the author explicitly requests that theme.
- Avoid jokes that make the animal seem cruel, aggressive, dangerous, or hostile when the photograph does not genuinely show that behavior.
- The joke should feel affectionate and funny rather than hard-edged.

## Image size and orientation

- Keep the finished asset similar to the supplied photograph's orientation, aspect ratio, and practical output size.
- Landscape source photographs should produce landscape posts. Portrait source photographs should produce portrait posts.
- Preserve the source composition when it already works. Crop only as needed for readable text and social-image safety.
- Do not force every post into 4:5, portrait, square, or the dimensions of a retained example.
- When generating at a lower social-ready resolution, choose the closest supported dimensions to the source aspect ratio. For a roughly 3:2 photograph, a result around `1536 × 1024` is appropriate.

## Dynamic color rule

- Derive each post's palette from the supplied photograph.
- Sample the dominant neutrals and one or two natural accents from the animal or scene.
- Maintain text contrast and legibility without imposing colors from a different episode.
- Do not treat the pale aqua, cream, navy, and coral of the pelican example as permanent brand colors.

Examples:

- A cloudy gull photograph can use cloud gray, warm white, charcoal, and restrained ochre-yellow from the bill and feet.
- A green field photograph may use grass green and the animal's natural accent colors.
- A coastal sunset photograph may use colors sampled from the sky, water, cliffs, or sand.

## Photograph-preservation rules

- Preserve the real animal, anatomy, markings, pose, expression, lighting, and authentic scene.
- Do not add, remove, duplicate, repaint, or materially alter wildlife unless the author explicitly requests a fictional edit.
- Do not invent props, landmarks, weather, or animal behavior to fill the composition.
- Text and restrained graphic decoration should occupy natural negative space whenever possible.
- Never cover the animal's face or important anatomical features with branding or copy.
- The animal remains the largest and strongest visual element. If the typography competes with the animal, reduce the complete text system and restore negative space.
- Treat “prominent” as legible and recognizable, not oversized. Adjust the series title, episode label, headline, and punchline together so the hierarchy stays balanced.

## Originality rule

For every new source photograph, create all three of the following from scratch:

1. An episode title connected to the specific photograph.
2. A concise visual joke based on what is genuinely visible.
3. Art direction that fits the source orientation, negative space, setting, and palette.

Do not recycle the visual concept, headline structure, punchline, or decorative motif from a previous episode merely because it worked once.

## Required X caption format

Use this exact structure, including the three emoji markers and both blank lines:

```text
Wildlife Meme Adventures 🧭

“[episode joke]”
📍 [verified location]
📅 [Month D, YYYY] · [Weekday, h:mm AM/PM]

Behind the meme: [factual description of the real photograph, followed by the light comic interpretation]
```

Example:

```text
Wildlife Meme Adventures 🧭

“No bars. Still five stars.”
📍 Mountains above Santa Barbara, California
📅 August 22, 2025 · Friday, 7:32 PM

Behind the meme: This is a real photograph taken from the mountains above Santa Barbara, looking across the chaparral ridges toward the city, coastline, Pacific Ocean, and distant islands - apparently beyond cellular service, but not beyond the rating system.
```

The markers are fixed: `🧭` follows the series name, `📍` begins the location line, and `📅` begins the date/time line. Keep one blank line after the series line and one blank line before `Behind the meme:`.

Do not replace this format with a conventional social caption. Do not add a promotional opening, closing call to action, or hashtag block unless the author explicitly requests one for that post.

The quoted second line is the episode's central joke. It may combine the image headline and punchline when that reads naturally.

The `Behind the meme:` line should first describe what the real photograph shows, then connect that fact to the joke. Keep the description truthful, compact, and conversational.

Use the photograph's capture metadata or the author's statement for the capture date and time. Do not use the export date, file-modification date, upload date, or post date as the capture date. Format a verified timestamp as `Month D, YYYY · Weekday, h:mm AM/PM`. If the time is not verified, omit the time rather than inventing it.

Use an author-provided or otherwise verified location. Do not add a more specific place than the available evidence supports. Use `Goleta, California` or another broader truthful label if a precise site is uncertain.

Do not invent a species identification. If the exact species is not verified, write `a gull`, `a bird`, or another accurate broader label.

Dates should be written clearly in captions, for example: `Photographed February 28, 2025.` Do not place a date or location label inside the image unless the episode's specific art direction calls for it and the facts are verified.
