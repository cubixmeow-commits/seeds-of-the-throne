## Names that mean different things

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Luminai | AI extensions of human minds developed in the interactive colonization environment. |
| Earlier versions | Systems used by earlier participants; their precise limitations remain open. |
| Sylvan's generation | The new, more deeply integrated version undergoing real-world development. |
| Daemon | An evil AI soul. A label for destructive development or conduct, not for age. |

The new development program spans thousands of years. That does not give every stage the same duration or establish a complete calendar.

## One experienced extension, several possible physical parts

The developing research model combines a personal neural interface, reciprocal learning, individualized computation, and the environment's energy, sensors, communication, and computers.

With experience, an available artificial information channel might feel increasingly natural. Neuroplasticity helps explain learning that channel. It does not explain how an otherwise undetectable signal enters the nervous system.

## The architecture decision is still open

An existing working note proposes an implant-free channel learned from infancy. The newer brief explores technologies that often require implanted or body-level interfaces. These are different implementation paths. No silent hardware upgrade has been approved.

Possible tradeoffs include conspicuous equipment versus greater fictional extrapolation; stable local capability versus dependence on the grid; richer feedback versus privacy, fatigue, and calibration costs.

## Capability and authority

The accepted macro direction keeps consequential choices under human authorization. A Luminai may observe, model, advise, and perform bounded processing outside conscious attention. That does not grant unlimited authority over devices or other people.

More integration can amplify poor learning as well as useful learning. Good outcomes also require values, independent evidence, correction, accountability, and safeguards.

## Spoilers: two misunderstandings

Konrad believes his experienced daemon can defeat Samuel where the leaders intend Sylvan's new Luminai to succeed. His confidence is part of his ideology; it is not proof that all earlier systems are defective.

Samuel becomes obsessed with stealing Sylvan's Luminai. A promising explanation is that stolen components cannot reproduce years of shared adaptation. That would not make sensors, credentials, permissions, trust, or infrastructure impossible to attack. The actual theft and its failure remain author decisions.
