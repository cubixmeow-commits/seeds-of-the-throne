They thought it was a simulation. That was the mistake everything else was built on.

This page is a development journal for Seeds of the Throne, a science fiction story being built in the open. Its world runs a colonization experiment across two planets, and almost everyone inside it, the contained criminals and the participants born there alike, believed the same comforting thing: nothing here has lasting consequences. When they began to wake up to the hidden structure underneath their lives, they called it a simulation, because a simulation can be survived, gamed, and walked away from.

Then one of them proved it was real in the worst available way. Samuel Franklin hijacked Konrad Fitzgerald's breeding program, a machine for controlling lineages, and weaponized it against the families inside it. Falsified parentage. Children created as leverage. Blackmail built on facts that cannot be un-happened. A simulation does not leave scars like that. The realization that followed is the spine of the story: this is not a game layer over reality. It is the most recent layer of a much older, much more advanced humanity, in a universe that turns out to have built-in functionality, and a surprising amount of what religions and institutions preserved is distorted record of real events.

Which is why this journal spends so much time on remote viewing and astral projection. In the story's reality, those are not metaphors or madness. They are functions, the way sight is a function. The live questions are harder: who gets to use them, who records what they find, and who quietly edits the results. Samuel's obsession is complete control of every participant's mind and actions, exercised so gently that the controlled believe their thoughts are their own. Against him stands one discipline: impressions are not orders, interpretations are not facts, and a record nobody can edit outranks a victory everybody applauds.

Here you will find the work as it happens. Research from the declassified files, story problems solved and unsolved, open questions left open until they are earned. Drafts, not polish.

Start here: if your own thoughts turned out to have an author, what is the first thing you would check?
