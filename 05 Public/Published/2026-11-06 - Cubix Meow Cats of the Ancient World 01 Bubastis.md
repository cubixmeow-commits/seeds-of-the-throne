## Caption as published

🐱🏺 CATS OF THE ANCIENT WORLD No.1: Where Cats Were Royalty

Mr. and Mrs. Meow have travelled back to Bubastis, city of Bastet, the cat goddess of ancient Egypt.

Here, cats were sacred. Herodotus wrote that when a household cat died, the family shaved their eyebrows in mourning, and harming a cat could be punished by death. In 1888, a cat cemetery at Beni Hassan held an estimated 80,000 mummified cats, buried with care for their journey into the next life.

The temple steps in this picture are not artistic license. Temples really were full of cats, fed, protected, and treated like honored guests.

Nine lives may have started here: nine was a sacred number in Egypt. Spain counts seven, Arabic and Turkish tradition six. How many does YOUR cat seem to have?

Next stop: the day cats decided a war. (Yes, really.)

THE OVERLAP: In the story world we are building, the temple keepers never ask: when everyone agrees the cats are sacred, who benefits when nobody questions it?
