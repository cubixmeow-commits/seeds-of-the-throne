Egypt, more than three thousand years ago. The tomb builders along the Nile believed a person was several things at once: a vital force, a heart, a name, a shadow, and the ba, drawn as a bird with a human head.

The ba could move. It left the tomb in the morning, traveled where it liked, and stood in the presence of the gods. At night it came back and settled on the body. Spell 89 of the Book of the Dead exists for that one reunion.

It is the oldest image in our archive of a part of a person leaving the body and returning. It is also the most misquoted. The ba flew only after death. It was never a traveler, and the Egyptians never treated it as a skill the living could learn.
