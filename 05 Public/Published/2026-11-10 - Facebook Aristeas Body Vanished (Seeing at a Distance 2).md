Around 2,700 years ago in Greece, a poet named Aristeas walked into a shop in his home town, fell down, and died. When his family came to collect the body, the shop was empty. That same day, a traveler swore he had met Aristeas alive on the road and spoken with him. Seven years later, Aristeas came home and wrote a poem about the far northern lands he said he had seen on his journey. Then he disappeared again. The historian Herodotus wrote the story down about 250 years later, and he is careful to say it is only what people told him. It may be the oldest story we have of someone leaving his body behind and traveling without it.
