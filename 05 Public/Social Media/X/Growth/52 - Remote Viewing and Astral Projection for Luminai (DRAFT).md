## 1. What the sources actually say

### 1a. Monroe: a first-person journal, then a training program

- Robert Monroe was a radio broadcasting executive with no parapsychology background. Beginning in 1958 he reported spontaneous experiences: a vibration moving through his body, then finding himself, by his account, floating apart from it, aware and looking back at his own sleeping form.
- *Journeys Out of the Body* (Doubleday, 1971) is his account of investigating the phenomenon on himself: part journal, part attempt to reverse-engineer a repeatable method. It is **one person's journal, not a clinical study and not a peer-reviewed classification**.
- The phenomenology he reports, in his terms: a vibrational state (mild tingling to electric buzz, full-body vibration with a roaring/buzzing sound), then separation of a "second body" that could move away from the bed, pass through walls, and travel to familiar places. His later books are *Far Journeys* (1985) and *Ultimate Journey* (1994); he died in 1995.
- He asked whether it could be taught. He founded the Monroe Institute (1971 as the Monroe Institute of Applied Sciences; renamed 1975) and developed **Hemi-Sync**, a binaural-beat audio technology intended to help listeners reach deeply relaxed states. The Institute's state map includes Focus 10 ("mind awake, body asleep") and Focus 12 (expanded awareness). The CIA's 1983 Gateway Process report assessed the Institute's training program; that assessment is a government document about a training method, not a validation of OBE as a physical fact.
- Usable story material, kept as phenomenology only: vibration as a threshold signal; mind-awake/body-asleep as a trainable state; a second-body frame for "where the perceiver is"; audio guidance as a technology that *presents* a state rather than commanding it. None of this establishes a mechanism.

### 1b. Targ and SRI: the Star Gate record, 1972-1995

- In 1972 physicists Russell Targ and Harold Puthoff at Stanford Research Institute (SRI) began psi research; they coined the term **remote viewing** to describe seeking impressions about a distant or unseen target using subjective means, differentiating it from clairvoyance. The CIA contracted them to create the program later known as Star Gate (originally codenamed Grill Flame; later Center Lane, Sun Streak, Star Gate).
- Timeline, per the published record: research and recruitment first; operational viewing for government clients from 1978 onward (CIA, DIA, FBI, Secret Service, NSA, Army intelligence and others). Targ left the project in 1982, saying its emphasis had shifted from research to classified operational work. Total funding is reported at roughly $20-25 million over 1972-1995.
- Method, in outline: a viewer is given minimal target information (in coordinate remote viewing, CRV, developed with Ingo Swann: geographic coordinates), then sketches and describes impressions of the target, ideally blind to its identity, with impressions recorded **before** feedback/reveal, and judged independently against the target. Targ defines RV as "an ability we all have to a greater or lesser degree, that lets us describe and experience activities or events blocked from ordinary perception by distance or time."
- The program was terminated in 1995. In July 1995 the CIA declassified and released program documents.
- Targ's *Third Eye Spies* (documentary 2018; book *Third Eye Spies: Learn Remote Viewing from the Masters*, 2023) tells the SRI story from the inside, including early successes the team reported (e.g. describing the NSA Sugar Grove station from coordinates, which Targ credits with securing the first CIA contract) and the shift to operational work. It is a participant's account, not a neutral history: reviewers note it omits the program's closure debate and downplays legitimate criticism of the early experiments.

### 1c. The 1995 evaluation: two reviewers, opposite conclusions, same numbers

- Before deciding whether to take the program over, the CIA commissioned the American Institutes for Research (AIR) to evaluate it: *An Evaluation of Remote Viewing: Research and Applications* (Mumford, Rose, Goslin, September 29, 1995). It assessed laboratory research and operational use separately.
- Statistician Jessica Utts (UC Davis) concluded psychic functioning had been scientifically established: across the best-controlled experiments the hit rate was about 34% where chance predicted 25% on a four-choice task, with very small p-values. She argued the effect was modest but consistent and comparable to effect sizes in accepted medical research.
- Psychologist Raymond Hyman (University of Oregon) agreed the statistics were anomalous and that later experiments had improved methodologically, but concluded anomaly alone did not prove a paranormal cause, no mechanism had been identified, and independent replication was required before drawing conclusions.
- The operational finding was blunt and both sides' context agrees on it: laboratory conditions did not transfer to intelligence work. Reports were vague, inconsistent, accurate mainly on broad background characteristics, and in no case had an RV report guided an actual intelligence operation. AIR recommended against continuing; the CIA accepted; Star Gate was terminated from the overt budget in 1995.
- This split verdict is the most story-useful fact in the whole record: **two fair-minded reviewers, same data, opposite conclusions about what the numbers mean.** It is the direct model for the "Two Realities" content format and for the story's own evidence problem (see §4).

## 2. RV vs. OBE: what the distinction is, in the sources

- **Remote viewing** is a task: describe a target you cannot see, under protocol, with a record made before feedback. It is defined by the procedure, not by the experience. A viewer need not feel they "went" anywhere.
- **Astral projection / OBE** is an experience: the subjective sense of leaving the body and traveling in a second body. It is defined by phenomenology, not by a scored target.
- Targ himself treated RV and OBE as related points on a single continuum of anomalous perception. Monroe's material is almost entirely on the OBE side; the SRI record is almost entirely on the RV side.
- For the story this maps cleanly onto the session's open question: RV is the *evidence* discipline (a protocol, a record, a score), OBE is the *experience* discipline (a state, a journey, a report). The Luminai question is which side the technology touches, and whether it can turn one into the other: a recorded OBE presented as evidence, or an RV protocol run through a Luminai as the presenting instrument. **No answer is adopted here.**

## 3. What is NOT established (honesty guardrails)

- Scientific reviews of the SRI and later experiments have found no credible evidence that remote viewing works as claimed; the topic is widely regarded as pseudoscience, and Targ's work has been criticized for lack of rigor. The 1995 evaluation did not establish a mechanism, and no mechanism has been identified since.
- Monroe's account is first-person testimony. Hemi-Sync is an audio-guidance technology for relaxation and attention states; its existence does not validate OBE.
- The declassified record proves the program existed, had budgets, personnel, taskings, and methods. It does not prove the phenomenon. Conflating "the government spent money on it" with "it worked" is the exact error the house style forbids.
- Content and story rules that follow: documents are documents, legends are labeled legends, misses are published, no outcome promises, no "proven" language. A Sealed Target reveal that scores honestly (including misses) is the format; a reveal that only ever succeeds is a tell.

## 4. Story questions this report can feed (all open, none resolved)

These are prompts for the author, not proposals for canon. Each maps to the 2026-11-04 working questions.

1. **Evidence vs. voice.** If a Luminai can present an RV impression or an OBE report directly to a mind, who authenticates it? The workshop's FP-01 question (who turns an observed problem into a rule) has an evidence cousin: who turns a *reported perception* into an accepted fact, and how is it challenged? A Luminai that presents is a witness; a Luminai that is obeyed is a ruler. The story's existing rule (AI proposes and verifies, humans decide) is a candidate boundary, not a decision.
2. **Latent vs. produced.** Three live options, kept separate: (a) capacities are latent human abilities the tech makes observable/trainable; (b) the tech produces them (wireless presentation simulating a second body / a target impression); (c) both, differently for different people. Each changes the story differently: (a) makes RV a human skill the criminals can also train; (b) makes every RV report suspect as possibly manufactured, which is the manufactured-synchronicity / counterfeit-interpretation machinery already in the story; (c) is the hardest to write and the most honest about the sources.
3. **RV as containment instrument.** Star Gate's operational failure is a plot resource: a containment regime that *uses* RV reports would inherit their vagueness and their need for interpretation, and interpretation is exactly where Samuel's fraud operates (cue, explanation, confirmation). A regime that *scores* RV honestly would need the same discipline as the Sealed Target: impressions before reveal, independent judging, published misses.
4. **The two layers.** Face-value unaware life vs. wireless tech presenting directly to the mind: an OBE in the unaware layer reads as spiritual experience; the same event in the tech layer reads as a presentation. The story can hold both readings at once, and the tension between them is the point. Do not collapse it by picking one early.
5. **Placement.** Per the session: add only if it changes a decision or contradiction already on the page. Candidate pages: the AI promise vs. tool reality in the opening; the unexpected result of exposing criminals (a perception that cannot be faked vs. one that can); CE-01 (the authentication boundary) after FP-01.

## 5. Content uses (for X / Facebook / Threads, once the author approves the report)

- **Sealed Target (lead format).** Protocol, adapted from the SRI discipline and the existing landscape research: post a target ID; a sealed photograph nobody has seen; audience posts impressions *before* the reveal; reveal with the photo and an honest score, misses included. Cadence decision still open (monthly per the manifesto text; quarterly was the alternative). First target should be a real photograph Noa has sealed, not a stock image.
- **Two Realities.** The 1995 split verdict as a format: same data, Utts vs. Hyman, reader judges. It is the story's epistemology in miniature and it is document-based, so it is safe to post.
- **Monroe strand.** Focus 10 / mind-awake-body-asleep as a trainable-state explainer; Hemi-Sync as audio technology; the 1983 Gateway assessment as a declassified-document post. Never present Monroe's journal as experiment.
