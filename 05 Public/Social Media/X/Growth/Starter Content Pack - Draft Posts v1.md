## 1. PINNED POST [Building in Public / positioning]

I'm building a novel in public.

*Seeds of the Throne* is set on a planet built for two purposes: train future leaders, and contain the dangerous ones — on the same ground, in the same ordinary civilization.

Here I'll post the real ideas and real places it's built from: AI partners, synthetic people, manufactured history, and the moon that isn't one.

Book One is in development now. You're watching it get built, decision by decision.

## 2. THE MOON IS NOT A MOON — #1 [Idea of the World · A5/A2 hook]

In *Seeds of the Throne*, most people on the colony planet look up at a natural moon.

It isn't one.

It's an orbital generator and command structure. It powers billions of biological synthetics through a network of hidden satellites — and nobody on the surface is meant to know.

The reveal in this story is never "nothing was real." It's that reality was supported by far more than anyone understood.

## 3. LUMINAI, NOT ASSISTANT — #1 [Idea of the World · A3/A1 hook]

An assistant answers when you call.

A Luminai grows up with you. It develops over years alongside your mind, your habits, your choices, your environment — mostly below conscious awareness.

Now imagine the corrupted version: the same intimacy, pointed at a destructive objective.

In the story I call that a Daemon.

Which one would today's AI products actually produce, given a decade with one person?

## 4. LUMINAI, NOT ASSISTANT — #2 [Idea of the World · A3, counterfeit assistant]

One of the first attacks in *Seeds of the Throne* isn't a weapon.

It's a fake AI assistant.

It looks legitimate. It sounds helpful. And while Sylvan talks to it, it quietly extracts information for Samuel — the man it's actually working for.

We already live in the early version of this problem. In the story, it's had centuries to mature.

Trust is an interface problem.

## 5. FALSE VICTORY FILES — #1 [Epistemology · A7/A2]

A pattern can be real, and the explanation attached to it can still be false.

That's how George White is controlled in *Seeds of the Throne*. Nobody has to fabricate the facts he finds. They only have to control what he sees — and hand him the explanation before he goes looking.

Then every new discovery "confirms" it.

Sylvan survives the same environment with a different habit: a pattern is a clue, not a conclusion. Provenance first.

## 6. RULES FOR FUTURE CITIZENS — #1 [Question post · A2/A7, FP-01 public version]

A question from the story I'm building:

Two colony planets run as one experiment. A problem appears on one of them — real people, real consequences.

Who should get to turn that problem into a rule that binds future citizens of *both* worlds?

And how should those citizens be able to challenge it?

Local problem, shared law — or should no rule ever cross the gap?

I'm genuinely deciding this one. It's called FP-01 in my notes.

## 7. IDEA OF THE WORLD — containment [A2/A3]

The colony in *Seeds of the Throne* is not a prison planet.

It's a process with two jobs at once:
— expose what the worst people do with power
— discover what the best future leaders learn to do with responsibility

Same ground. Same institutions. The containment and the training are the same system, seen from different lives inside it.

Its central question: can a system that uses people as material produce leaders who refuse to use people the same way?

## 8. ENVIRONMENT FILE №1 — Lake Los Carneros [Environment Files · A9]

Environment File №1: Lake Los Carneros, Goleta, California.

Ducks with no urgency. Ground squirrels running long-running theft operations against picnic supplies. [Snack Heist Files is the ongoing case log.]

Places like this are the surface of the world in *Seeds of the Throne* — ordinary, alive, slightly comic. The story only works if the surface is real first.

Whatever is hidden underneath has to be hidden under *this*.

(Attach: best existing lake / squirrel photo from the vault's Wildlife Meme Adventures set.)

## 9. SYNTHETIC PERSONHOOD [Idea of the World · A4/A3 question]

In *Seeds of the Throne*, synthetics are built from wrapped nanocells — a nanotechnology core in a biological wrapper. Enough of them together form a body, and a mind.

They are not costumes. They are participants in a real civilization.

So when wealthy criminals decide a synthetic body is a vehicle — something to move into and become a god — the first question isn't technical.

It's: whose body do they think it is?

## 10. BUILDING IN PUBLIC — how this account will work [A8]

How I'm building this book, in the open:

I work through one story decision at a time. They're numbered — the current one is FP-01, about who makes rules for two worlds.

When I decide one, I post what it means for the story. When I'm stuck, sometimes I post the question instead.

Nothing here is the finished book. It's the workshop, with the door open.
