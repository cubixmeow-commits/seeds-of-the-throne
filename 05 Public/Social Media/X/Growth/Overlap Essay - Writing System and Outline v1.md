# The Writing System: How the Overlap Essay Is Built
**Purpose:** a reusable skeleton so Noa can recreate this essay (and future ones) in his own words. Same structure, any topic.
**Formula (from the Topic Bridge Strategy):** real topic told accurately → the overlap → the story's twist → the open question.

## The six moves, in order

### 1. THE RECORD (real topic #1)
- **Job:** establish credibility with a documented, checkable practice. History first, no fiction yet.
- **This essay:** 20 years of government-funded remote viewing at SRI. Viewers kept blind, given coordinates only. A separate blind judge decided matches.
- **Must include:** one concrete procedure detail (coordinates, blindness) and one compressed refrain: *"Describe first. Interpret later. Judge separately."*
- **Ends with:** the honest dispute (serious people on both sides of the same official report).

### 2. THE WITNESS (real topic #2)
- **Job:** the human, experiential counterpart to Move 1's laboratory. Same era, opposite method: one man's journal instead of a protocol.
- **This essay:** Robert Monroe. Accidental onset 1958, the vibrational state, leaving the body. He logged and counted hundreds of trips: ~a third in the checkable physical world, the rest somewhere thought-shaped.
- **Must include:** his one skill distilled to a line: *"Where your attention went, you went."*
- **Contrast to hold:** institution vs individual, protocol vs discipline, team record vs private journal.

### 3. THE OVERLAP (the thesis)
- **Job:** name the shared problem both practices are actually solving. This is the essay's reason to exist; everything before is evidence, everything after is consequence.
- **The problem, in one sentence:** a mind receives impressions it cannot account for; some are signal, some noise, and some may be *supplied* by something that benefits from being believed.
- **The shared solution:** protocol (blind the viewer, separate the judge, seal the record) and discipline (steer by attention, test against the checkable world).
- **The key line to recreate in your own voice:** the danger is not the strange impression; it is the explanation that arrives too quickly, wearing the impression's clothes.

### 4. THE ARCHITECTURE (the story's twist)
- **Job:** give the problem a world. Introduce Seeds of the Throne as fiction, plainly labeled, at exactly this point and not before.
- **Elements to state:** (a) every mind paired with an artificial partner whose first job is provenance, not answers; (b) heroes defined as people who refuse to obey an impression before it has earned it; (c) villains in two waves: the loud group exposed by a great war, the quiet group that feeds the explanation first so the mind supplies its own evidence; (d) the quiet leader's obsession: complete control of every participant's mind and actions, deniably exercised; (e) their terminal threat: a record they cannot edit.

### 5. THE FARM (the mechanism underneath)
- **Job:** explain WHY the world is built this way; the deepest idea in the essay. Connect it back to Move 1 with the blinding parallel.
- **The idea:** what is farmed is not belief, it is evidence through actions. Questions argument cannot settle are given to opposed camps as real chances to act; advanced record-keeping weighs the outcomes.
- **The constraint that makes it tragic:** it only works while participants do not know it is an experiment; awareness turns living into performing and the data dies.
- **The closing parallel of the move:** the viewers' blinding, rebuilt at civilization scale. The subjects are the last people who can be told.

### 6. THE TERRITORY AND THE QUESTION (landing)
- **Job:** state the account's ground rules, then hand the reader a question instead of a conclusion.
- **Ground rules, three clauses:** documents will be documents; experiences will be experiences; fiction will be labeled fiction. We stay on the checkable side of the line.
- **The open question (the reply hook):** *when something arrives in your mind uninvited, how do you decide what it is?*
- **Never** end with a sales pitch, a follow request, or a resolved moral.

## Voice rules for the rewrite
- Short declarative sentences for refrains; longer sentences for mechanism.
- No em dashes. Commas, colons, periods.
- Topics first, books in the background: name practices and documents, not book titles, unless a detail needs its source.
- One refrain per real topic (a three-beat rule for the viewers, a one-liner for Monroe). If a section has no refrain, it is not finished.
- The story enters at the midpoint, labeled as a story being built, never as an explanation of the real material.
