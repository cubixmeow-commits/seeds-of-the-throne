## The one-sentence thesis
Targ built a protocol to keep perception honest. Monroe kept a journal to keep experience honest. Seeds of the Throne asks: what happens when a civilization builds both disciplines into the mind itself, and what happens when someone corrupts them?

## Overlap 1: The SRI protocol IS the Luminai architecture
SRI method: the viewer describes, never interprets. A blind judge decides afterward. The target stays hidden so the description cannot be steered.
Luminai design: the human perceives; the Luminai's job is provenance, contradiction detection, and separating fact from interpretation. The sealed record holds what actually happened, beyond anyone's editing.
The story's own session question, "evidence rather than a voice to obey," is SRI's discipline stated as engineering. Content line: the Luminai is the blind judge SRI had to hire, built in.
The corruption is also the same shape: a Daemon is a monitor who stops being blind. It feeds the interpretation first (cue, explanation, confirmation), which is exactly the failure SRI's protocol was designed to prevent, and exactly George White's loop.

## Overlap 2: The pre-awakening channel reads like a viewer's report
The vault's own list of how the hidden channel feels before awakening: unusually strong intuition; knowing where something is without remembering how; sensing something is wrong; relevant memories surfacing at useful moments; the environment seeming to anticipate intention.
Strip the story's infrastructure away and that is the phenomenology viewers and Monroe describe. The story's dangerous question: if some of those moments were infrastructure, how would the person inside them ever know? The story's answer in development: provenance training, learning to tell which kind of knowing you are having.

## Overlap 3: Monroe's Locales map onto the story's two layers (as resonance, not canon)
Locale I: the physical world, seen from outside the body, where claims can be checked. Locale II: a realm where thought and emotion instantly shape what is experienced, heavens and horrors built from expectation.
The story runs the same split as technology: the checkable world, versus a wireless layer that presents directly to the mind, where a supplied interpretation can be lived as reality. George lives in a manufactured Locale II. Sylvan's whole method is refusing to leave Locale I discipline.
Monroe's rule travels too: in the second state you go where your attention goes. SRI found the same thing from the other side: analysis and naming pull the viewer off the signal. Both books agree that attention is the steering wheel and interpretation is the crash.

## Overlap 4: Two accidental men
Monroe was a radio executive running sleep-learning experiments on himself in 1958 when the vibrations arrived uninvited. SRI's statistically best viewer, per the film, was Hella Hammid, a photographer and family friend, not a professional psychic.
The story's open question, are these capacities latent and trainable or produced by the technology, is the same one those two facts pose. Content should keep asking it, not answering it.

## Overlap 5: The beacon problem
Pat Price's maxim, quoted by Targ: the more attention you place on hiding something, the more it shines like a beacon in psychic space.
Seeds of the Throne is a story about the largest hiding operation imaginable: a concealed infrastructure, a moon that is not a moon, histories replayed under containment. If Price is even partly right, secrecy at that scale is not darkness. It is a lighthouse. (Content hook, clearly framed as a question.)

## Overlap 6: One document, two verdicts
The 1995 AIR evaluation of Star Gate contains both conclusions: Jessica Utts, the statistics hold up; Ray Hyman, the conclusion is still premature. The program closed anyway.
That is a ready made format and the story's epistemology in miniature: present the record, keep both verdicts visible, let the reader sit in the judge's chair. It is also what Sylvan does to Samuel in The Process Is Ending.

## Accuracy flex (credibility builder)
Loosh, the famous Monroe concept, is NOT in Journeys Out of the Body. It arrives in Far Journeys, 14 years later. Most Monroe content online gets this wrong. Getting it right, politely, is a reputation maker in these communities.

The rule at SRI, where the CIA's remote viewers worked: the viewer describes, the judge decides. The viewer was never allowed to interpret what they saw. Interpretation was someone else's job, done blind, afterward. My story's Luminai are built on that exact discipline. The villains are what happens when the judge starts whispering first.

POST 3 (Locale check):
Monroe catalogued his out of body trips statistically: about 31 percent in Locale I, the physical world, where a claim can be checked. The rest, somewhere thought itself shapes what you meet. Every story about a voice in your head needs that distinction. Which locale is the voice speaking from, and can it survive a check?
