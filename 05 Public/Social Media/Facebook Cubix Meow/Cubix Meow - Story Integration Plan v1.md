# Cubix Meow × Seeds of the Throne — Story Integration Plan v1

*Written 2026-11-06, after the launch of Cats of the Ancient World №1 (Bubastis) and №2 (Pelusium), both published the same evening at Noa's direction. This plan supersedes the staging in the earlier Overlap Plan: the pivot launched faster than the original phased schedule, so this version is built around what is now live. All story references are thematic only. No cats enter the story; no post presents fiction as fact.*

## What the page is becoming

Cubix Meow keeps its identity: wholesome travels of Mr. and Mrs. Meow, original illustrations, positivity welcome. Onto that chassis, one change: **the travels now go somewhere with a past.** Each destination carries a real, sourced piece of cat history, and most carry an Overlap: a single closing question or statement that belongs to the Seeds of the Throne world. Over months, the page's center of gravity shifts from "cute cats in places" to "cats at the crossroads of history and mystery," which is where the story's future readers already live. The page never announces a rebrand. The audience discovers the depth gradually, the way the Meows discover it.

## The three content lanes and their ratios

Target mix once at full rhythm (3 posts per week):

1. **Ancient World installments (about half).** The flagship series. Numbered, collectible, always: illustration of the Meows on location, sourced history in the caption, one engagement question, an Overlap coda (see rules below).
2. **Classic Cubix Meow (about a third).** The page's existing comfort content: the cast (Maya, Tony, Angus) on travels, art styles, seasonal and cozy posts. This lane protects the existing audience and the monetization. It never carries an Overlap.
3. **Wonder posts (the remainder).** Short, lighter bridges that need no story knowledge: cats and the night sky (the page already did Astronomy Day and has an observatory profile image), cats sensing storms and earthquakes, cats in libraries and archives. These quietly train the audience toward the story's perception themes: what animals notice that we miss.

## The Overlap: rules of engagement

- One Overlap per Ancient World post, always last, always under the same "THE OVERLAP:" label. Consistency is what turns a coda into a ritual.
- It is a question or a single stark statement drawn from a story theme, never exposition, never character names from the novel, never a plot summary.
- It must be earnable from the history above it: the reader should feel the history raised the question, not that an ad was attached.
- Rotation of themes, in the order the audience can absorb them:
  1. **Sacred authority** (№1: who benefits when nobody questions it?)
  2. **Belief as vulnerability** (№2: standing behind what your enemy holds sacred)
  3. **Nine lives / recognition** (№3, below): how you recognize the same soul in a new life, by face or by habits. This is Recurrence, the story's deepest theme, arriving disguised as folklore.
  4. **Manufactured devotion** (votive mummies): when sacred objects are mass produced, what exactly is being sold?
  5. **Animals perceive more** (cats sensing what instruments miss): the doorway to the remote viewing themes, months before those words appear.
  6. **Who keeps the records** (pawprints in the archive): evidence, provenance, sealed histories.
- Only after at least 8 installments does any post name Seeds of the Throne or link the Atlas. Before that, the phrase is always "the story world we are building." Curiosity first, attribution second.

## Series queue: the next six installments (№3 to №8)

3. **Nine Lives, Counted Carefully.** Egypt 9, Spain 7, Arabic and Turkish tradition 6, and why the numbers differ. Illustration: the Meows meeting three versions of a very familiar looking cat. Overlap theme 3 (recognition). This is the emotional centerpiece of the early series; schedule it after №1 and №2 have had a week to breathe.
4. **The Mummy Workshops of Bubastis.** The votive industry: sacred cats by the thousands, and what X-rays found inside some of them. Overlap theme 4 (manufactured devotion). Tone care: wonder, not gore.
5. **The Cat Who Guarded the Library.** Temple and monastery cats, scroll guardians, and the 15th century Dubrovnik manuscript with a cat's pawprints across the page. Overlap theme 6 (records). The pawprint post is inherently shareable.
6. **Cats Who See in the Dark.** What cat senses actually do (hearing ranges, night vision, whiskers reading air currents), told accurately, then the folklore of cats staring at empty corners. Overlap theme 5 (perception). First Wonder/Ancient hybrid.
7. **The Goddess Changes Her Name.** Bastet becomes Artemis's companion, Diana turns into a cat in Ovid. Gods merging and changing form across cultures. Overlap: identity that survives a change of form (bridges toward the transfer theme, still unnamed).
8. **The Beckoning Cat.** Gotokuji temple and the maneki-neko legend: a cat's raised paw saves a lord from lightning, or beckons fortune, depending who tells it. Overlap: signs and luck, and how a gesture becomes a symbol everyone trusts.

After №8: review metrics, then decide on naming the story and linking the Atlas, per the rule above.

## Cadence and production

- **Rhythm:** 3 posts per week (for example Mon/Wed/Fri evenings Pacific), mixing lanes per the ratios. Ancient World installments no more than twice a week; scarcity keeps them events.
- **Pipeline per post:** history nugget verified against sources (legend labeled as legend) → illustration generated in the established style with the Meows' identity markers (his backpack, her flower) → caption drafted under 1,024 characters, no em dashes, history first, Overlap last → Noa approves → publish → record saved in the vault at `05 Public/Published/` with the post link.
- **Reusable assets:** series title block, the Overlap label, and a standard closing question format. Captions get written in batches of two so a week is never blocked on drafting.

## Guardrails (protecting monetization and trust)

- The page stays cat first in name, bio, and majority content. The existing Intro line about AI and sleeping minds already points the right way; no bio change until after №8 review.
- History is accurate and sourced; legend is labeled legend every time (№2 set the precedent). This is the page's credibility moat and the story's brand in miniature.
- No post claims or hints that the fiction explains real history, real religion, or real cats. (Vault Report 49's own boundary.)
- Watch two numbers weekly: unfollows and per post engagement. A spike in unfollows after an Overlap post means slow the rotation, not abandon it: the next two posts go pure Classic lane.
- If Facebook flags AI content labeling, keep it: several page posts already carry the AI label and the audience has not objected.

## Checkpoints

- **After №4 (about week 2):** compare engagement on Ancient World posts vs the page's pre pivot baseline (28 day views 338, engagements 3 at audit). Any installment beating baseline views by 3x becomes a format template.
- **After №8 (about week 4 to 5):** decision point, with Noa: name Seeds of the Throne on the page, add one Atlas link line to the About, and start cross posting mirror versions to Instagram (@cubixmeow). Also decide whether a second series (Surface Archive, real Goleta wildlife photography under a "surface record" frame) joins as the photography lane.
- **Ongoing:** every published post is archived in the vault the same day, so the page's history and the story's public record never diverge.

## What this buys the story

By the time Book One gates start resolving in public, Cubix Meow's audience will already have spent months enjoying exactly the questions the novel asks: who runs the temples, what belief costs, how you recognize a soul, who keeps the records. They will not need the story explained to them. They will have been rehearsing for it, happily, one cat at a time.
