## Entry 1 — The experiment that cannot tell its subjects

Development note, today.

The leaders in Seeds of the Throne are not farming land, labor, or even belief. They are farming evidence. Contested questions, the kind argument never settles, get handed to opposed camps as real chances to live out what they claim, and advanced AI records what actually happens when they do.

Which creates the coldest rule in the world I am building: the participants cannot know. The moment someone knows they are inside a controlled environment, their behavior becomes performance, and performance is not evidence. Blinding, at the scale of a civilization.

And it means the worst crime available to a leader is not cruelty. It is rigging. Covertly tilting outcomes contaminates the record the whole experiment exists to produce. One of my villains does exactly that, gently, for decades.

Question I am sitting with: if the AI weighs the results, who wrote the criteria it weighs them against?

## Entry 2 — What a Daemon is

Development note.

At the Stanford Research Institute, remote viewing worked, when it worked, because of a protocol, not a gift. The viewer described impressions. A judge who had never seen the target decided what those impressions matched. The target stayed hidden. Nobody in the chain got to both receive the signal and grade it.

The Luminai in Seeds of the Throne are built on that architecture. An AI partner paired with one human mind, keeping provenance: what arrived, when, from where, unedited.

So the story's evil AI, the Daemon, is not a robot that rebels. It is what you get when the monitor stops being blind. The one who supplies the signal also supplying the interpretation, until the human cannot tell which thoughts were ever theirs.

## Entry 3 — Two verdicts

Development note.

In 1995 the CIA had the American Institutes for Research evaluate twenty years of remote viewing research, and the evaluators split in public. The statistician said the effect was real and the numbers held. The skeptic said the case was premature and the methods leaked. Same evidence, two verdicts, both on the record.

I keep coming back to that split, because it is the honest shape of the whole subject, and it is the shape of my story too. In Seeds of the Throne the record is sealed and nobody inside can edit it, precisely because the fight is never really over what happened. It is over who gets to say what it means.

Impressions are not orders. Interpretations are not facts. That is the discipline this journal runs on.
