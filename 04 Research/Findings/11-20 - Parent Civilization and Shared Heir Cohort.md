# Parent Civilization and Shared Heir Cohort — Findings Map

## Core synthesis

The legitimate leaders' project is most plausible when it is designed around five protections:

1. **Agency:** heirs repeatedly gain the right and information needed to author their own futures.
2. **Capability:** preparation creates real competence through consequential but bounded responsibility.
3. **Belonging:** family love, peers, friendship, and identity remain available independently of performance.
4. **Fairness:** conditions are interpreted in context, assistance is visible, and consequential judgments can be appealed.
5. **Stopping authority:** no philosophy can justify unlimited harm merely by claiming that the evidence is incomplete.

## Findings with the strongest creative value

### Preparation should expand choice

A useful distinction across the series is that preparation increases the number of choices a person can responsibly make, while manufacture narrows the person until only the designer's preferred choice remains psychologically available.

This gives the legitimate parents and the Throne opposite methods even when both claim to be preparing heirs.

### Happiness is part of readiness

The heir's emotional health, relationships, curiosity, capacity for joy, and life beyond office are not distractions from leadership. They help protect independent judgment. A person whose entire meaning depends upon one promised inheritance is unusually vulnerable to control through delay, threat, and destiny.

### Shared struggle needs repeated interdependence

One traumatic ordeal would create brittle solidarity and could encourage an elite survival cult. The stronger mechanism is repeated cooperation, changing roles, mutual witness, common goals, and opportunities to see how peers behave when status reverses.

### Dual identity is safer than forced unity

The heirs should remain members of distinct empires while also belonging to the planetary cohort. A shared identity that erases subgroup identity risks assimilation, resistance, or allowing one empire to define the whole civilization in its own image.

### Ancient founders must change their empires too

The heirs cannot solve every problem created by centuries of founder dependence. Loving rulers prepare for succession by distributing functions, separating personal preference from law, creating honorable lives outside inheritance, and developing post-rule roles that cannot silently reclaim command.

### Consent must grow with understanding

Parental permission at entry is insufficient. The strongest architecture uses affirmative assent, renewed adult consent, independent advocacy, meaningful partial refusal, and viable lives after withdrawal. Operational details may remain concealed; love, rights, purpose, and the existence of alternatives should not be lies.

### Accommodation must be judged by behavior and capability

Negotiation and inclusion can support genuine moderation. They become dangerous when they increase an aggressor's resources or expectation of victory without changing the underlying objective. Evidence of change should be costly and behavioral: enforcing limits on insiders, protecting dissent, accepting disadvantageous boundaries, and cooperating without immediate reward.

### Fairness is not identical treatment

The process should ask what capacity an environment actually measures. Equal conditions can be unfair when irrelevant differences determine access. Multidimensional records are more credible than one total score and less likely to become a new hierarchy available for capture.

### Good disagreement needs ownership

Reconciliation, Boundary, Cultivation, and Evidentiary leaders can remain morally serious without agreeing. Their governance requires protected minority reports, shared evidence, divided institutional roles, explicit decision rules, and clear responsibility for cumulative outcomes. The Throne's opportunity lies in gaps no school owns.

### A century requires stopping rules

Long duration can reveal succession, institutional capture, and strategic adaptation. It does not justify observing until a destructive plan reaches completion. Independent monitors need authority to stop for harm, success, or futility, and some prohibited methods can become clear before long-term outcomes are statistically settled.

### The cohort must remain accountable to non-heirs

Mutual trust among future sovereigns can prevent conflict, but it can also create an insulated cartel. Ordinary planetary communities need representation, access to records, and standing to challenge the cohort's preferred account of its own formation.
