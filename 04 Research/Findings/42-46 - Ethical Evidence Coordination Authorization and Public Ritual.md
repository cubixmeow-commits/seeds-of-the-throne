# Ethical Evidence, Coordination, Authorization, and Public Ritual — Synthesis of Reports 42–46

## Finding

The five research tracks reinforce one combined endgame architecture:

`private consent-scoped evidence -> independently verified pattern -> predictions fixed before outcomes -> protected matching among isolated targets -> bounded public common knowledge -> multi-role human authorization -> durable witnessed record`

This is research-derived story guidance, not canon. It improves the current possibilities but does not select the final event, permission system, evidence, or descendant participation model.

## What materially changed

### 1. Ethical proof is two-layered

The public case does not need public parentage. Identity-bearing evidence can remain sealed while independent custodians verify non-identifying claims, aggregate patterns, and command relationships. Consent should be scoped by field, audience, use, and duration.

**Improves:** P-04 and P-09.

**Protects:** descendant agency, privacy, and the prohibition against genealogical spectacle.

### 2. The failure ledger needs a prediction lock

Failures become difficult to rewrite only when success criteria, observation windows, responsibility, and confidence are recorded before outcomes. Revisions should remain possible but append-only. The strongest visible pattern is not repeated failure alone; it is repeated definition drift after failure.

**Improves:** P-02 and P-07.

**Protects:** uncertainty and legitimate learning; Sylvan still lacks perfect observability.

### 3. Common knowledge is a separate dramatic threshold

Sending the same private evidence to many leaders does not necessarily let them coordinate. A safe system can first match confidential reports, then let participants opt into protected coordination, then create a bounded public event where each sees that the others received the same authenticated fact.

**Improves:** P-04 and the public function of P-01.

**Protects:** first movers from premature exposure and avoids an instant faction-wide reversal.

### 4. Human authorization is a grammar, not a master key

Mature systems distinguish identity, role, access, request, authorization, concurrence, execution, verification, audit, and override. Samuel can possess broad access yet still lack an independent legitimate human decision required for a consequential act.

**Improves:** P-05, P-06, and SC-010 setup possibilities.

**Protects:** bounded alpha power, human responsibility, and the rule against a magical moral refusal by synthetics.

### 5. The ceremony should reverse through its own steps

Public political ritual establishes legitimacy through recognized sequences, witnesses, documents, declarations, and shared custody. The strongest coronation/audit does not bolt evidence onto a spectacle; every step meant to establish Konrad's authority exposes a failed premise or invalid authority claim.

**Improves:** P-01 and its possible combination with P-02 and P-04.

**Protects:** exact venue, chronology, and private evidence from premature definition.

## Strong combined possibility

Without selecting it, the research makes one combination especially coherent:

1. descendants and targeted leaders deposit consent-scoped fragments privately;
2. independent custodians detect a system-wide pattern without disclosing identities;
3. Samuel and Konrad commit to observable predictions or authority claims before the public event;
4. the ceremony's own sequence presents bounded, independently verified contradictions;
5. witnesses see the same proof and one another's recognition of it;
6. one consequential task remains available but requires separate legitimate human authorizations;
7. refusal, attempted bypass, and explanation drift enter the witnessed audit record.

This combination could unite P-01, P-02, P-04, P-05, and P-09. It also risks overloading one event and therefore requires an author decision before structural development.

## Recommended author questions

1. Should the surrender spectacle and first bounded audit share one ritual architecture, or remain separate events?
2. If shared, which single proof family leads the reversal: failed predictions, false separation, or systemic lineage abuse?
3. Does descendant-controlled evidence remain entirely behind independent custodians during the first public reversal, or do some adults choose a limited visible role?
4. Is the cooperation requirement best dramatized as one multi-role operational act, or as a longer pattern of legitimate shared decisions?

Ask only one at a time. The first remains the current recommended gate.
