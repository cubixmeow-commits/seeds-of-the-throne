# Luminai Evidence Audit and Architecture Boundaries

## Scope and confidence

This is a targeted primary-source audit of the preliminary September 4 brief. It is not an exhaustive review of neuroscience or wireless engineering. Verified below means the stated narrow finding was checked in the primary paper or its indexed abstract. It does not certify the full paper, a medical application, or the fictional architecture.

The preserved brief is [[04 Research/Full Reports/48 - Preliminary Neuroplastic Luminai and Wireless Grid Brief]]. Its recommendations remain proposals. The author has approved the broad developmental direction, not each piece of hardware or each security claim.

## Corrections to the preliminary synthesis

1. **Transduction first, learning second.** Neuroplasticity can explain adaptation to available signals. It cannot make the brain directly receive arbitrary information without a physical mechanism. The existing [[02 Story/Components/Learned Wireless Cognitive Channel]] deliberately chooses a speculative implant-free mechanism; interface research must not silently overwrite it.
2. **Power is not data or stimulation.** Room power, body links, neural recording, and sensory feedback need separate constraints. The environment can plausibly supply context without extracting every fact from the brain, but this is a proposed system design.
3. **Acoustic coupling matters.** The brief's drones/furniture ultrasound idea is not supported as an airborne, through-skull implant link by the cited neural-dust result. Treat it as unverified; a close tissue-coupled interface is a different proposal.
4. **No automatic security theorem.** Individualized decoders and a learned vocabulary can make stolen components less useful. They do not prove credentials cannot be copied, context cannot be poisoned, or people cannot be coerced. An explicit threat model is still needed.
5. **No moral upgrade theorem.** Plasticity and deeper integration can strengthen harmful loops. Accountability requires values, evidence, independent review, refusal, and enforceable limits.
6. **Comparative success is not a controlled experiment.** Sylvan and Konrad differ in person, training, permissions, support, goals, and history as well as generation. A successful outcome can support a bounded next development step without isolating architecture as the sole cause.

## Candidate architectures, all unapproved

| Path | Narrative benefit | Research and story cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Preserve implant-free field learning | Retains the existing invisible infancy channel. | Requires an explicit fictional transduction breakthrough; adult skill learning is insufficient evidence. |
| Discreet personal physical interface | Clearer connection to recording/stimulation research. | Reopens the no-implant rule; consent, maintenance, visibility, and long-term tissue compatibility need decisions. |
| Hybrid sensory/body interface plus environmental inference | Reduces what needs decoding directly from the brain. | Still needs reliable individualized feedback and privacy boundaries; less magical precision. |
| Stage-dependent architecture | Could distinguish early training from Sylvan's deeper phase. | Adds complexity and cannot be used to evade the author gate over physical implementation. |

## Unverified leads retained from the brief

The full brief's other citations are research leads, not verified support in this integration. The companion reference-status inventory identifies every URL and whether it was relied on. The 2021 handwriting paper's publisher retrieval failed; its numerical claim was not reused as a verified result. Tactile stimulation, nanodiscs, cortical microchip networks, body-coupled power, injectable arrays, prosthesis embodiment, and the PNAS learning papers need their own participant/species and method checks before further promotion.

## Next research sequence

- Resolve whether the author wants to retain implant-free transduction before choosing the technical direction.
- Compare one realistic recording channel and one feedback channel, including signal drift and maintenance.
- Define local/offline operation, power and data budgets, outage behavior, and moon oversight latency at story-relevant scale.
- Build a threat model separating the bond, personal model, permissions, sensors, and institution.
- Test the proposed safeguards in a scene where a technically valid instruction must still be refused.

These are advisory research tasks routed through the existing workshop and weekly intake, not a second execution list.
