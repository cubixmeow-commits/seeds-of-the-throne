# 42 — Ethical Disclosure Without Genealogical Spectacle

**Story focus:** P-04 Common Knowledge Without Public Parentage; P-09 The Children Decline the Role

> Research supports the story but does not establish story facts. Historical institutions and real victims remain research sources only; story translation must use invented people, structures, and events.

## Research question

How can an investigation prove a systemic pattern of reproductive or identity abuse while minimizing involuntary disclosure of individual parentage?

## Bottom line

The strongest model separates **proof of the pattern** from **disclosure of identities**.

Credible investigations can establish that a system existed through aggregate records, recurring fields, independent custodians, authenticated samples, and representative testimony without publishing a complete identity graph. Consent should be specific to the information, audience, purpose, and duration of use. Identifiers should be stored separately from testimony or pattern data, with access restricted to people who need it. Public proceedings can coexist with confidential or closed evidence channels when safety, dignity, or privacy require them.

The resulting Seeds mechanism should not ask descendants to exchange privacy for credibility. It should let them decide which facts travel, while an independent process verifies that the private evidence genuinely supports the public pattern.

## Supported findings

### 1. Survivor authority is procedural, not decorative

**[SUPPORTED]** Survivor-centered investigation guidance places safety, dignity, informed consent, confidentiality, transparency, and meaningful choice at the center of decisions affecting a survivor. Consent is not one blanket yes/no event; investigators should record what use and sharing the person actually authorized.

**Story value:** descendants can choose different disclosure envelopes. Participation does not require public appearance, public parentage, or permission for every later use.

### 2. Confidential evidence can still support public findings

**[SUPPORTED]** OHCHR guidance allows public hearings to build understanding and trust while preserving closed or confidential testimony where necessary. Secure information systems, controlled access, and separation of personal identifiers from interview records reduce exposure.

**Story value:** Sylvan's public case can present verified propositions and pattern counts while the underlying identity-bearing records remain sealed and reviewable by a bounded authority.

### 3. Pattern proof can be assembled from many standardized records

**[SUPPORTED]** Truth-commission information systems have converted large numbers of statements into consistently coded records so investigators could test for systematic patterns while retaining the underlying statements for verification. Aggregate, non-identifying information can serve oversight and public-accountability purposes without publishing personal records.

**Story value:** repeated non-identifying fields—dates, authorization classes, routing signatures, intervention types, or custody transitions—can prove coordination across many cases.

### 4. Genetic information is relational

**[SUPPORTED]** Genetic information can reveal facts about relatives who never submitted a sample. National Academies and genomics guidance therefore emphasize informed use, confidentiality, limited access, and consent for redisclosure. Forensic DNA guidance also warns against presenting genetic identification as infallible.

**Story value:** no single participant can ethically authorize publication of an entire family map. Genetic evidence should confirm only the minimum proposition necessary, with non-genetic administrative evidence carrying as much of the public case as possible.

## Research-to-story translation

`scoped consent + separated identifiers + aggregate pattern analysis + independent verification -> descendants retain control -> the organization-wide operation becomes provable without a public genealogy -> Sylvan must accept a less spectacular but more legitimate presentation`

### Strong candidate mechanism: the two-layer proof

1. **Private verification layer:** identity-bearing records, samples, and family relationships remain sealed with multiple independent custodians.
2. **Public pattern layer:** the audience receives non-identifying claims, counts, time relationships, command signatures, and a statement of which private evidence independently verifies each claim.
3. **Consent ledger:** every contributor's permissions specify what can be verified, shared, quoted, displayed, or withdrawn.
4. **Challenge path:** an authorized reviewer can test a public claim against the sealed record without disclosing the person behind it.

This is a plausible extrapolation from current practice, not an established Seeds system.

## Constraints and failure modes

- Anonymized data can sometimes be re-identified when combined with other records.
- Aggregate proof may still expose a small or unique family unless fields are carefully bounded.
- A public audience may wrongly treat sealed evidence as untrustworthy unless the custodians and verification process were established earlier.
- One descendant cannot represent tens of thousands of people.
- Consent under dependency, coercion, or information asymmetry may not be meaningful.
- Genetic evidence should not become a moral ranking system or the only proof of abuse.

## What this adds to the creative packet

- P-04 becomes a **two-layer evidence architecture**, not just anonymized statistics.
- P-09 gains a concrete power: descendants can define disclosure envelopes and force Sylvan to redesign the proof around their boundaries.
- P-01's public audit can remain dramatic while displaying verified pattern claims rather than faces, names, or family trees.
