# 44 — Common Knowledge Against Distributed Blackmail

**Story focus:** P-04 Common Knowledge Without Public Parentage

## Research question

What communication structures let isolated targets discover a shared coercion pattern and coordinate safely when each fears being the first to disclose?

## Bottom line

The key transition is not from ignorance to information. It is from **private knowledge** to **safe mutual discovery**, then to **common knowledge** that others have received and recognized the same bounded fact.

Private reports can be held in escrow until an independently verifiable match or threshold exists. Participants can then be contacted without immediately revealing them to one another or to the public. A later synchronized, public, or mutually witnessed act can create the common knowledge needed for coordination. These phases should remain separate because the structure that safely discovers a pattern is not necessarily the structure that publicly proves it.

## Supported findings

### 1. Common knowledge changes coordination

**[SUPPORTED]** Experiments distinguish private knowledge, shared knowledge, and common knowledge. People attempt risky coordination more often when information is publicly salient and each participant can infer that others know that others know it.

**Story value:** sending every leader the same private dossier is insufficient. Each may still fear that others will deny, defect, or expose the first mover.

### 2. Concealed matching can discover a shared perpetrator before public disclosure

**[SUPPORTED / ORGANIZATIONAL EXAMPLE]** Callisto's matching-escrow model was designed to let disconnected survivors confidentially record a perpetrator and receive support when another report matched, without requiring the first reporter to make an immediate public accusation. The model separates record creation, match detection, human assistance, and the survivor's later choice.

**Story value:** isolated lineage targets can learn that the pattern is shared without Samuel learning who first moved or the system automatically publishing their evidence.

### 3. Public signals coordinate, but can also miscoordinate

**[SUPPORTED]** Coordination research shows that salient public signals can become focal points. It also warns that a wrong or noisy public signal can coordinate people around error. Common knowledge amplifies a signal's social effect; it does not guarantee the signal is true.

**Story value:** the bounded claims must be independently authenticated before the public phase. Sylvan cannot substitute spectacle for verification.

### 4. Anti-retaliation design needs multiple reporting channels

**[SUPPORTED]** Whistleblower guidance recommends confidential or anonymous reporting paths, protection from retaliation, clear limits on confidentiality, and trusted officials or ombuds channels.

**Story value:** participants should have more than one custodian or channel, so Samuel cannot defeat the coalition by controlling a single intake point.

## A four-stage story architecture

1. **Private deposit:** each target places a bounded fragment with a trusted custodian and defines consent.
2. **Threshold match:** the system identifies repeated command signatures, dates, claims, or intervention patterns without revealing identities.
3. **Protected mutual recognition:** matched participants learn that corroboration exists and choose whether to coordinate through intermediaries.
4. **Common-knowledge event:** a synchronized statement, witnessed verification, or public ritual makes the authenticated pattern mutually visible.

No stage automatically authorizes the next.

## Research-to-story translation

`confidential deposits + threshold match + independent authentication + synchronized bounded disclosure -> isolated targets know they are not alone and know the others know it -> Samuel's individualized blackmail loses coordination power without a public genealogy dump`

## Constraints and failure modes

- Matching criteria can create false positives or leak identifying details.
- A compromised intermediary can map the network for the coercer.
- Participants may share a fact but still disagree about action, risk, or responsibility.
- Publicity can trigger retaliation before protection exists.
- A threshold system must not silently decide credibility or guilt.
- A single broadcast creates salience only if the audience trusts the verification and knows who else received it.

## What this adds to the creative packet

- P-04 gains a staged transition from isolated fragments to protected matching to public common knowledge.
- The public event's function is not merely disclosure; it lets each targeted leader see that the others have received the same authenticated proposition.
- Sylvan's leadership choice becomes temporal: he must delay the spectacular reveal until participants have protection and a coordination channel.
