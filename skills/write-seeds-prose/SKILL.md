# Write Seeds Prose

## Select the mode

Choose one mode before writing:

- **Exploratory draft:** Write a complete scene to discover and test possibilities. Use this by default when the author asks to write a scene without assigning it manuscript authority.
- **Manuscript candidate:** Write prose intended to survive into the book. Use only when the author explicitly requests a manuscript candidate or promotes an exploratory draft.
- **Continuation:** Continue directly from author-supplied or approved prose while preserving its viewpoint, tense, distance, voice, and authority level.
- **Revision:** Diagnose and revise submitted prose or a prior draft to the degree the author requests. Preserve purposeful unusual choices.

If the author wants questions, exercises, or short comparative demonstrations, hand off to `coach-seeds-writing`. If the request requires inventing or choosing story events before prose can be written, hand off to `develop-story-session`. If factual consistency is the main issue, hand off to `check-story-continuity`.

## Establish authority and scene pressure

Read `03 Context/RULES.md`, the relevant character and world notes, and any author-written sample identified for the task. If no sample exists, use the current defaults cautiously:

- conversation rather than lecture;
- explanations remain partial, motivated, and limited by the speaker's knowledge;
- first-person present tense is a working mode, not a permanent rule;
- the author's final fiction remains author-controlled;
- do not use em dashes.

Use `references/prose-checklist.md` for drafting and revision.

Before drafting, determine:

1. whose experience controls the passage;
2. what that character wants now;
3. what the other characters want, withhold, or misread;
4. what pressure changes by the end;
5. which facts are established, working, proposed, or unresolved.

Infer these from the request and vault when safe. Ask one focused question only when an unresolved choice would materially change the scene.

## Draft

1. Establish whose experience controls the passage and what that person wants now.
2. Limit perception and language to what the viewpoint can know, notice, or misunderstand.
3. Put world information under pressure from action, desire, conflict, or consequence.
4. Let dialogue pursue goals, conceal information, and reveal relationships indirectly.
5. Favor concrete sensory and procedural detail over abstract explanation.
6. Preserve ambiguity when the story depends on manufactured reality or incomplete evidence.

For an exploratory draft:

- label the output **Exploratory draft — non-canon**;
- write a coherent scene rather than a menu of fragments;
- identify proposed connective details outside the prose;
- allow provisional choices that help the scene function, but never present them as vault facts;
- treat polish as exploration, not approval.

For a manuscript candidate:

- label the output **Manuscript candidate — requires author approval**;
- use settled facts and choices the author has explicitly approved for the passage;
- do not solve unresolved story questions merely to complete the prose;
- state any remaining authority risk outside the prose.

For a continuation, inherit the source passage's authority unless the author changes it. For revision, explain the main revision pressure briefly before presenting the revised passage.

When alternatives are useful, produce no more than three and vary a consequential craft choice rather than vocabulary alone.

## Refine and promote

Treat exploratory drafting as the beginning of a refinement loop:

1. Receive the author's reactions as controlling evidence about intention and voice.
2. Identify the scene-level change requested before revising sentences.
3. Revise the entire affected scene so pacing, motivation, and consequences remain coherent.
4. Preserve earlier alternatives when the author has not rejected them; do not silently collapse possibilities into canon.
5. Continue refining until the scene's dramatic movement and language support the same intention.
6. Promote the result to manuscript-candidate status only when the author explicitly requests it.

Promotion changes prose authority, not story canon. Route newly approved events, mechanics, or character facts through `develop-story-session` before recording them as canon.

## Revise

Preserve the author's distinctive choices unless asked to transform them. Diagnose before rewriting. Remove generic grandeur, repeated conclusions, false profundity, exposition disguised as dialogue, and phrases that explain an image after it already works. Prefer whole-scene coherence over accumulating local patches.

Mark invented connective material as proposed when drafting from incomplete story notes. Do not smuggle new canon into polished language.

## Guardrails

- Do not imitate a living author's style.
- Do not convert faction prejudice into neutral narration.
- Do not make manipulated premises erase a character's remaining agency or responsibility.
- Do not let Luminai or Daemon become omniscient without established support.
- Do not use finished prose to settle an unresolved story decision.
- Do not treat polish, repetition, or author enthusiasm as automatic promotion to manuscript or canon.
