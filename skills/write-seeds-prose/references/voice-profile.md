# Seeds Voice Profile

This is a live project profile, subordinate to `03 Context/WRITING-STYLE.md` and direct author instruction.

## Core voice

The prose should feel like an impossible history becoming legible under pressure. It is controlled rather than florid, readable rather than cryptic, and ominous because facts accumulate into implications, not because the narrator continually announces dread.

## Preferred qualities

- Clean, confident sentences.
- Specific institutional and physical detail.
- Calm narration around disturbing material.
- Unequal information between characters, reader, and historical record.
- Contradictions that remain active long enough to create curiosity.
- Irony generated by motives, records, procedures, and outcomes.
- Emotional force expressed through choices, attention, avoidance, bodily behavior, and changed interpretation.
- Technical systems treated as lived infrastructure rather than spectacle.

## Registers

### Archive thriller
Use for reconstructed history, evidence, records, false public explanations, later reinterpretation, institutional causality, and long-range consequence.

### Close psychological
Use when a character's perception matters more than the later historical record. Keep external reality uncertain when appropriate while making subjective experience precise.

### Political grotesque
Use for contained leadership and corrupt institutions. Let vanity, euphemism, procedure, family status, jealousy, cowardice, and routine management carry the horror. Avoid cartoon villain monologues.

### Immediate scene prose
Use concrete action, spatial logic, dialogue, interruptions, objects, and consequences. Do not turn every scene into documentary summary.

## The turn

A turn changes the meaning of what came before. It may be a final fact, reinterpretation, contradiction, or consequence. It should not become a mandatory ending device. If several recent sections end with a punchline-like reversal, choose a quieter consequence instead.

## Negative profile

Avoid:

- generic prestige-thriller language;
- faux-biblical grandeur;
- constant statements that something was "never really" what it seemed;
- repetitive "not X, but Y" constructions;
- excessive single-sentence paragraphs;
- omniscient moral commentary inside close POV;
- villains explaining their ideology for the audience;
- summary paragraphs that repeat what the scene just demonstrated;
- metaphors that exist only to make a sentence sound literary;
- abstract nouns replacing observable behavior.

## Calibration rule

When uncertain, become more concrete, more character-motivated, and slightly less explanatory.
