# Tracks Pass

Use this pass when prose has moved through outlines, brainstorming, critique, or AI-assisted revision. Its job is to remove residue from the development process without erasing useful ambiguity or intentional framing.

## Look for residue

- process narration such as “as established,” “we need to understand,” or “this section explores”;
- outline labels, option menus, critic instructions, TODO language, and decision scaffolding left inside narrative;
- explanations that describe what the passage is doing after it has already done it;
- prototype orientation repeated after the reader is oriented;
- transitions that refer to earlier drafts, prompts, notes, or the writing process;
- summary language copied from a scene packet instead of rendered through action, perception, evidence, or consequence.

## Preserve intentional framing

Do not remove a phrase merely because it is procedural. Records, hearings, archives, institutional instructions, and development labels can be part of the intended form. Public development pages may also explain status or uncertainty on purpose.

For each suspected track, ask:

1. Is the phrase addressed to the reader, a character, or the development team?
2. Does the final form require that address?
3. If removed, is any necessary fact, status label, or orientation lost?

Remove or rewrite only residue whose original development job is finished.

## Mode boundary

- **Development prototype:** flag tracks that make the test harder to judge. Do not polish disposable prose merely to hide its origin.
- **Manuscript candidate:** remove development scaffolding unless the fiction deliberately presents it as a record or artifact.
- **Public project prose:** retain useful status, provenance, and uncertainty labels while removing internal instructions and author-facing option menus.

Report the specific residue and its effect. Do not use this pass as a general rewrite.
