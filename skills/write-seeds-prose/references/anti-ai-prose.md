# Anti-AI Prose Pass

The purpose is not to make prose artificially irregular. It is to catch recurring model habits that weaken Seeds.

## High-priority failure patterns

### Explanation after effect
A gesture, image, contradiction, or line lands, then narration explains what it means. Cut the explanation unless it adds genuinely new interpretation.

### Thesis dialogue
Characters state the scene's theme, history, or moral instead of pursuing a goal.

### Rhetorical templates
Watch repeated constructions such as:

- "not X, but Y";
- "It wasn't X. It was Y.";
- "The question was not...";
- three-item escalations used automatically;
- repeated sentence fragments as drama;
- repeated one-line ominous paragraph endings.

A single use can work. Repetition reveals the machine.

### Generic grandeur
Words such as ancient, impossible, terrible, vast, perfect, inevitable, monstrous, destiny, truth, and power should earn their force through specific context rather than substitute for it.

### Abstract emotion
Replace labels like dread, rage, confusion, devastation, and unease when behavior, perception, attention, bodily response, or choice would be more precise.

### Symmetrical paragraphing
Avoid every paragraph having similar length, similar internal rhythm, and a neat final sentence. Let structure follow thought and action.

### False profundity
Delete statements that sound important but add no new fact, choice, image, relationship, or interpretation.

### Lore gravity
The model may pull every scene toward the coolest system concept in the vault. Resist. The scene belongs to the character's immediate experience.

### Premature closure
Do not resolve ambiguity merely because polished prose feels more complete with an answer.

### Villain performance
Avoid villains who constantly advertise cruelty, superiority, or ideology. Petty logistics, rationalization, irritation, fear, and self-interest are often stronger.

## Mechanical scan

After drafting, search mentally or with tools when available for:

- em dashes;
- repeated uncommon words;
- repeated first three words of sentences;
- clusters of fragments;
- clusters of one-sentence paragraphs;
- repeated contrast templates;
- multiple closing turns within a short span.

Mechanical flags are prompts to inspect, not automatic errors.

## Final question

Could a competent generic model have produced this passage without knowing who these people are, what they want, and how this world works? If yes, increase character-specific causality and concrete specificity before increasing ornament.
