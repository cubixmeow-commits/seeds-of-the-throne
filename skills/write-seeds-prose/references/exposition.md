# Exposition and World Systems

## Rule

Exposition is strongest when it changes what a character can do, what they believe, what something costs, or how evidence must be interpreted.

## Delivery channels

Prefer, in roughly this order when appropriate:

- consequence;
- procedure in use;
- object or environment;
- disagreement;
- failed expectation;
- record/evidence;
- instruction with a real purpose;
- brief narration.

Summary narration is valid when dramatizing the information would be slower and less interesting. Do not turn "show, don't tell" into a prohibition on efficient narration.

## Technology

People living with advanced systems should treat them as infrastructure. Describe what a system permits, prevents, costs, hides, misreports, or fails to do before explaining its architecture.

Avoid marveling language unless the viewpoint is genuinely encountering something unfamiliar.

## Archive-thriller exposition

Useful pattern:

1. accepted account;
2. surviving detail;
3. contradiction;
4. later interpretation;
5. consequence.

Do not mechanically use all five every time.

## Explanation budget

Before adding explanation ask:

- Does the reader need this now?
- Does the POV have access to it?
- Does knowing it improve the current dramatic beat?
- Can one concrete consequence replace three explanatory sentences?

Stop once the reader can follow the present stakes. Curiosity is allowed to remain.

## Common failures

- encyclopedia paragraphs attached to a scene;
- characters explaining shared knowledge;
- narrator defining project terminology before it matters;
- explaining mechanisms that are intentionally uncertain;
- repeating information in narration after dialogue already established it;
- turning every mystery into a technical briefing.
