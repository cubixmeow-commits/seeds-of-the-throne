# Spoken Clarity — Final Prose Candidates

**Status:** Provisional craft notes for comparative tests. They do not change `03 Context/WRITING-STYLE.md`, establish a manuscript register, or replace the Development Prototype Style. Use only when refining accessibility and scene movement in an original Seeds passage. Do not imitate another author's voice, recurring jokes, narrator persona, or sentence pattern.

## Listening test

Read the passage aloud once without stopping. Ask whether a listener can identify who is speaking, what the viewpoint character wants now, what changed, and which uncertainty remains. Repair a missing causal link or speaker cue before shortening sentences. An audible passage can still be quiet, procedural, or formally reconstructed; it need not sound like someone addressing the reader.

## Technical claim to usable meaning

When an unfamiliar system matters, consider a short sequence: a technical claim or observed behavior; the viewpoint character's **provisional** plain-language understanding; a decision, risk, or refusal affected by it. The character's understanding is not narrator-certified truth. Let later evidence correct it when appropriate. Omit a layer if the consequence already makes the claim legible, and do not repeat this sequence at fixed intervals.

## Dialogue and concrete language

Let an explanation emerge when speakers disagree about an action, cost, permission, or interpretation. Give each speaker a purpose beyond teaching the reader. Use the terms of the viewpoint character's actual work and life when those terms sharpen a decision; avoid generic military banter or a fixed specialist-and-translator pair. A Luminai can have precision and limits without becoming a comic heckler. Humor may expose a mistaken status claim, but do not carry a bit past the harm it reveals.

## Compression without erasing evidence

Begin near the pressure when preparation and travel would only repeat known logistics. Keep procedure, waiting, transit, or aftermath when it changes evidence, a relationship, a choice, or the cost of continuing. Do not treat every scene as a seven-beat problem loop or require an irreversible event in its first line.

## How to test before adopting

Compare two short versions of the **same already-defined scene** using the same facts and outcome: one in the current close viewpoint and one with more spoken clarity. Preserve each version's knowledge boundary. Ask the author which makes the causal turn easier to follow and the character more specific; also note what each loses. Promote a rule only after it improves several materially different scenes without flattening silence, records, uncertainty, or controlled historical reconstruction. Record any accepted preference through the skill's Learning loop; leave these candidates provisional until then.
