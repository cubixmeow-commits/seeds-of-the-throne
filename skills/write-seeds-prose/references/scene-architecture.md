# Scene Architecture

## Governing principle

A scene is a change produced under pressure. Information alone is not a scene.

## Minimum scene engine

1. **Entry condition**: what is already in motion when the scene begins?
2. **Immediate want**: what does the POV character want before the scene ends?
3. **Friction**: person, institution, environment, evidence, body, clock, or misunderstanding obstructs that want.
4. **Tactic**: the character does something to overcome the friction.
5. **Response**: reality answers the tactic.
6. **Escalation or reorientation**: the character must change approach or interpretation.
7. **Exit condition**: choice, consequence, discovery, loss, commitment, reversal, or altered relationship.

Do not force a formula if the scene already has movement, but diagnose missing movement with these beats.

## Pressure types

Use one or combine several:

- deadline;
- social exposure;
- institutional procedure;
- physical risk;
- evidence contradiction;
- loyalty conflict;
- status threat;
- resource loss;
- unwanted witness;
- information asymmetry;
- false assumption becoming costly.

## Scene openings

Prefer beginning near an active disturbance rather than with setup. Establish orientation through selected details that matter to the current want.

Avoid opening with generalized weather, biography, world history, or a thesis about the character unless that information is itself under pressure.

## Scene middles

Do not merely increase volume. Change tactics, information, alliances, costs, or interpretation. A scene can become quieter while becoming more dangerous.

## Scene endings

Strong exits include:

- a decision that commits the character;
- a consequence that cannot be undone;
- evidence that changes interpretation;
- a relationship shift;
- a new cost;
- a newly credible threat;
- a question made sharper rather than answered.

Avoid explaining the scene's meaning after the decisive beat.

## Multi-scene rhythm

Do not make every scene climax the same way. Alternate discovery, confrontation, procedural pressure, aftermath, pursuit, intimate conflict, historical reconstruction, and strategic decision according to story need.
