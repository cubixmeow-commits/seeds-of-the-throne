# Prose Evaluation Rubric

Use this to diagnose and compare drafts. Scores are aids to judgment, not canon or aesthetic truth.

## Critical gates

A passage cannot be considered ready if it:

- presents unresolved or invented story material as established fact;
- leaks knowledge unavailable to the POV or chosen register;
- contradicts a direct current author instruction;
- revives a superseded project style without explicit permission;
- contains em dashes;
- explains away an ambiguity the story intentionally preserves.

## 100-point rubric

### Dramatic movement — 15
Does the passage change pressure, choice, relationship, evidence, or interpretation? Information alone earns little credit.

### Character agency and motive — 10
Are actions caused by what characters want, fear, protect, hide, or misunderstand?

### POV integrity — 10
Does perception remain inside the permitted knowledge and psychic distance?

### Dialogue and subtext — 10
Do lines pursue goals and alter the exchange rather than deliver lore?

### Exposition discipline — 10
Is world information necessary now, motivated, and integrated through consequence, procedure, conflict, evidence, or efficient narration?

### Information control and suspense — 10
Are knowledge gaps, false beliefs, clues, and reveals managed deliberately?

### Physical and procedural specificity — 10
Does the scene inhabit a credible place with usable objects, bodies, timing, procedures, records, distance, or consequences?

### Seeds voice and register — 10
Does the passage fit the current live style while selecting the right register for the material?

### Anti-AI distinctiveness — 10
Does the prose avoid generic grandeur, rhetorical templates, repeated turns, false profundity, symmetrical cadence, and explanation after effect?

### Sentence and paragraph control — 5
Do syntax, paragraph length, emphasis, and rhythm follow thought and pressure rather than a uniform pattern?

## Interpretation

- **90–100**: strong candidate for author-level review; improve only where a specific gain is visible.
- **80–89**: solid draft with one or two meaningful weaknesses.
- **70–79**: functional but still noticeably shaped by craft problems.
- **Below 70**: revisit scene design or POV before line polishing.

Do not chase a numerical score by adding more description, conflict, or style devices. A quiet scene can score highly if every choice is deliberate.

## Refinement protocol

1. Preserve the two strongest dimensions.
2. Identify the lowest one or two dimensions that actually matter to the scene.
3. Make one structural revision addressing them.
4. Re-score.
5. Run the anti-AI pass.
6. Stop when further changes become preference swaps rather than clear improvements.

## Comparison rule

When comparing versions, explain *why* one works better in terms of reader experience and dramatic function. Never choose a version solely because its total score is one or two points higher.
